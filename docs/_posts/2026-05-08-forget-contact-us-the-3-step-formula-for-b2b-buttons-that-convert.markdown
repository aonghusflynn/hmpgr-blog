Most B2B websites suffer from a "ghost town" effect. 

Visitors leave because your Call to Action (CTA) is intimidating or boring. Vague buttons like "Contact Us" or "Submit" kill conversions. They sound like work and imply a sales pitch is coming.

Change how you ask for the click to get more leads. Fix your buttons with this three-step formula.

## 1. Swap vague verbs for value verbs

Your button text should tell the user exactly what happens next. "Contact Us" is a process. "Get My Free Audit" is a result. When writing your CTA, focus on what the user gets instead of what they must do.

### Examples of value-driven shifts
*   Instead of "Learn More": Use "See How It Works."
*   Instead of "Sign Up": Use "Start Your Free Trial."
*   Instead of "Book a Call": Use "Claim Your Strategy Session."

## 2. Eliminate commitment phobia

B2B buyers fear wasting time. They worry that clicking a button leads to endless sales emails or a high-pressure phone call.

Lower this barrier with clarity. If your trial does not require a credit card, say that. If your demo only takes 15 minutes, mention it.

### How to reduce friction
*   Use "No credit card required" near the button.
*   Use "Join 5,000+ marketing managers" to show social proof.
*   Be clear about the time investment.

## 3. Master the visual hierarchy

If everything on your page is shouting, nothing is heard. Your primary CTA should be the most distinct element on the screen.

### The contrast rule
Use your button color only for the actions you want people to take. If your brand color is blue and your whole site is blue, a blue button disappears. Use a high-contrast accent color like orange or green to make it pop.

### White space is your friend
Don't crowd your buttons with text or images. Give the button room to breathe. This draws the eye naturally to the center of the action.

## Pro tip: Use click triggers

A click trigger is a small piece of text placed near your button. It addresses the user's final hesitation. 

Try these:
*   "Takes less than 2 minutes."
*   "Cancel anytime."
*   "No obligation."

These phrases can increase click-through rates because they handle objections before they happen.
