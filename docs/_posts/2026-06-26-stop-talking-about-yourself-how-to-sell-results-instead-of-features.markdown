Most B2B websites make the same mistake. They act like a digital brochure for the company's ego. 

Founders and marketing managers spend months perfecting descriptions of their "proprietary algorithms" or "complex infrastructure." They focus on the *what* and the *how*. 

But your customers? They only care about the *why*. Specifically, why does your product make their life easier or their business more profitable?

If you want to increase your conversion rate, you need to stop talking about yourself and start talking about results.

## The feature trap

A feature is what your product does. A result is what your customer achieves.

B2B buyers are under pressure. They have budgets to justify and bosses to impress. They look for ways to save ten hours a week on reporting so they can go home on time rather than "cloud-based AI-driven analytics."

When you lead with features, you force the visitor to do the mental heavy lifting. You are asking them to figure out how your "API-first architecture" actually helps them. Most won't bother. They will just leave.

## How to flip the script

Rewriting your website for results requires a shift in perspective instead of a total overhaul. Use this three-step framework to audit your current copy.

### 1. Identify the "so what?"
Look at every heading on your homepage. After reading it, ask yourself: "So what?" 

If your headline is "Our Dashboard has Real-Time Sync," the "so what" is that your customer never has to worry about making decisions based on old data. The result is **confidence and accuracy.** Use that instead.

### 2. Focus on the outcome
Your website should paint a picture of two worlds: the "Before" (the pain) and the "After" (the win). 

*   **Before:** Manual data entry and lost leads.
*   **After:** Automated workflows and higher revenue.

Your product is simply the bridge between those two worlds. Describe the bridge, but sell the destination.

### 3. Use customer language
Stop using internal jargon. If your team calls a feature "The Multi-Node Integration Hub," but your customers call it "the thing that connects my CRM," use their words. 

Customer language is naturally outcome-oriented. It's simple and direct.

## The "results-first" checklist

To see if your website is ready to convert, check it against these criteria:
*   **The Hero Section:** Does it promise a specific outcome within the first 5 seconds?
*   **The Sub-headers:** Do they describe benefits or just list tools?
*   **The Call to Action:** Does it lead to a solution (e.g., "Start Saving Time") or just a mechanical step (e.g., "Submit Form")?

### Pro tip: the "rule of you"
Scan your homepage and count how many times you use the words "We," "Our," or "Us" versus "You" and "Your." If your company name appears more often than your customer's needs, your copy is backwards. Aim for a 2:1 ratio in favor of "You."
