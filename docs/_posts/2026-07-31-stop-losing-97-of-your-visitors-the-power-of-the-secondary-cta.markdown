Most B2B websites make a common mistake. 

They treat every visitor like they are ready to buy right now. They plaster "Book a Demo" or "Contact Sales" everywhere. 

But data shows that about 97% of your visitors aren't ready to talk to sales yet. They are still learning. They are looking for a solution, but they aren't ready for a meeting.

If you only offer one "hard" call to action (CTA), those people will simply leave. To grow, you need to offer them a different path.

## Why "demo or nothing" fails

When you only have a high-friction CTA, you create a binary choice. The visitor either commits to a conversation or they walk away.

Think of it like walking into a store. If a salesperson immediately asks for your credit card, you'll probably walk out. You want to browse first. 

B2B buyers are the same. They need to build trust with your brand before they give you their time. A secondary CTA captures the "browsers" and keeps them in your world.

## Choosing the right secondary CTA

A secondary CTA should be "low friction." It requires very little effort or commitment from the user. 

### Examples of low-friction offers
*   **A "how it works" video:** Let them see the product without a live guide.
*   **A checklist or template:** Give them a tool they can use immediately.
*   **A comparison guide:** Help them see how you differ from competitors.
*   **An interactive tool:** A calculator or a free audit that provides instant value.

The goal is to provide value first. You want the visitor to think, "If their free stuff is this good, their paid product must be incredible."

## How to place your CTAs

Your primary CTA (the big ask) should still be the most visible. It usually sits in your top right navigation and the center of your hero section.

Your secondary CTA should support the primary one. 

1.  **Use an outline button:** If your primary button is a solid color, make the secondary one an "outline" or "ghost" button. 
2.  **Place it side-by-side:** Put your "Watch video" button right next to your "Get started" button.
3.  **The mid-page pivot:** As the user scrolls down and learns more, offer the secondary CTA again. They might not be ready for a demo, but they might want that whitepaper.

## Pro tip: the "no-email" lead magnet

Try offering a high-value resource that doesn't even require an email address. This sounds counterintuitive. However, by removing the form entirely, you build brand affinity. Use a retargeting pixel to show ads to these visitors later. You will find that these "frictionless" visitors often come back to book a demo on their own.
