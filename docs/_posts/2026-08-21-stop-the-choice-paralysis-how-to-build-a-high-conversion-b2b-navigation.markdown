Most B2B websites treat their navigation menu like a junk drawer. 

They cram in every product feature and company milestone. This is a mistake. 

When you give a visitor too many choices, they choose nothing. They get overwhelmed and leave. 

Simplify the journey to improve your results. Audit and fix your navigation for better conversion with these steps.

## The rule of seven

The human brain struggles with long lists. Once you hit more than seven items in a menu, the information starts to blur. 

Your goal is to make the next step obvious. If your top header has 12 different links, you are forcing the user to work. They should be able to click without thinking.

### Audit your current menu
Look at your header. Remove anything that does not help a prospect understand what you do or how to buy it. 

Move "Careers," "Press Releases," and "Privacy Policy" to the footer. These are important, but conversion drivers deserve the primary space. Keep the top for the links that generate revenue.

## Speak human, not corporate

B2B companies often use clever names for their features. They use words like "Solutions," "Ecosystems," or "Methodologies." 

Avoid this. Use labels that your customers use. 

If you sell project management software for architects, call the page "Project Management" instead of "Interdisciplinary Collaboration Hub." 

Clear labels beat clever ones.

## The "money button" strategy

Every page on your site should have one primary goal. In your navigation, this is usually your "Request a Demo" or "Start Free Trial" button.

This button must be visually distinct. 
* Use a high-contrast color. 
* Place it in the top right corner where users expect to find it. 
* Make sure it stays visible as the user scrolls.

Distinguish the "Contact Us" button from your "Blog" link to show which is more important.

### Pro tip: the "footer safety net"
The header guides navigation while the footer supports undecided visitors. Use your footer for an expanded site map, social links, and secondary resources. This keeps your header clean so power-users can still find deeper content.

## Guide the journey with sub-menus

If you have a complex product, use "Mega Menus" sparingly. Use icons and short descriptions to explain what each category means instead of a wall of text.

Group your features by benefit or persona. 
Example: Instead of ten features, group them under:
* "For Marketing Teams"
* "For Sales Teams"
* "For Operations"

This helps your visitor find the right solution faster.
