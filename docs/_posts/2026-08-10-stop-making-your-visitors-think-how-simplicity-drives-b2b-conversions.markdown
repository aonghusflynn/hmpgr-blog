B2B buyers are overwhelmed. They juggle meetings, budgets, and software options. When they land on your website, they want a solution.

Most B2B websites fail because they are too complex. They use big words to sound "enterprise-ready." They pack the navigation with twenty different links. 

The result? Your visitors get "cognitive overload." When the brain has to work too hard to understand what you do, it chooses the easiest path: leaving. Simplify your website to turn more visitors into customers.

## Speak like a human, not a brand manual

Jargon is a conversion killer. Terms like "synergistic ecosystem" or "next-generation paradigm" mean nothing to a tired marketing manager.

Complex language makes you feel safe, but it makes your reader feel confused. Confusion is the opposite of a sale.

### Actionable steps
*   **Audit your headlines:** Remove every word that ends in "-ize" or "-ation" if it doesn't add specific value.
*   **The "bar test":** Read your copy out loud. If you wouldn't say it to a friend at a bar, don't put it on your website.
*   **Focus on the "so what":** Say "Your site won't crash when traffic spikes" instead of "We offer cloud-native scalability."

## Ruthlessly prune your navigation

Your navigation menu is a map. 

When you give a visitor ten options in the header, they often choose none. They get paralyzed by choice. This is known as Hick's Law. The more choices you offer, the longer it takes to make a decision.

### Actionable steps
*   **Limit to five:** Aim for five or fewer items in your main navigation.
*   **Hide the "about" page:** Unless you are a personal brand, "About Us" is rarely a high-conversion page. Move it to the footer.
*   **Highlight one path:** Your "Book a Demo" or "Start Free Trial" button should be the only thing that stands out.

## Clarify the after state

B2B buyers buy a better version of their workday. 

Show what their life looks like after they use your software. If your tool saves them ten hours a week, lead with that. 

### Actionable steps
*   **Use "result" imagery:** Show a photo of a relaxed team or a growth chart pointing up instead of a screenshot of your dashboard.
*   **Swap features for benefits:** Try "Get answers in minutes, not days" instead of "24/7 Support."

### Pro tip: the squint test
Blur your eyes and look at your homepage. If you can't tell what you do and what the main button is within three seconds, your page is too cluttered. High-converting sites have a clear visual hierarchy that guides the eye even when the text is unreadable.
