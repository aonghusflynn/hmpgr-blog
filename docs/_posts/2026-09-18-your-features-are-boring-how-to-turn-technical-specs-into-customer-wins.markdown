Most B2B websites make the same mistake. They talk about themselves. They list technical specifications, internal project names, and complex modules. 

Your customers care about their problems, not your features. 

To increase your conversion rate, sell what your product does for the customer rather than what it is.

## The "so what?" test
Every time you write a line of copy for your website, ask yourself: "So what?"

If your headline says, "We have a 99.9% uptime guarantee," the customer thinks, "So what?" You must answer that thought immediately. 

**The fix:** "99.9% uptime means your team never loses a day of productivity."

### Translate technical specs into emotional gains
B2B buyers are human. Even in SaaS, they want to gain time or avoid pain. 

- **Technical Spec:** "Cloud-native architecture."
- **Customer Win:** "Access your data from anywhere without installing clunky software."

- **Technical Spec:** "API-first integration."
- **Customer Win:** "Connect your existing tools in minutes, not weeks."

## Sell the result
Your website visitors want a solution. They are at Point A (a problem) and want to get to Point B. 

Your product is the bridge. Don't spend your time describing the paint on the bridge. Show them the view from the other side.

### Use the benefit-first layout
When designing your feature sections, lead with the outcome. 

1. **The Header:** State the win (e.g., "Cut your reporting time in half").
2. **The Sub-header:** Explain the feature that makes it possible (e.g., "Our automated data aggregator pulls from every source instantly").
3. **The Proof:** A quick stat or a small screenshot of the result.

## The pro tip: the negative space strategy
### Pro tip
Tell customers what they lose by not using your product. In B2B, the fear of wasted budget or missed deadlines motivates buyers. Use loss aversion in your copy. Instead of "Save $500 a month," try "Stop wasting $500 a month on inefficient manual entry."

## Focus on clarity over cleverness
Clever headlines often confuse people. Confused people do not click "Sign Up."

Keep your language simple. Use the same words your customers use in support tickets or sales calls. If they call it a "headache," you call it a "headache." 

When you speak their language, you build trust. Trust is the foundation of every B2B conversion.
