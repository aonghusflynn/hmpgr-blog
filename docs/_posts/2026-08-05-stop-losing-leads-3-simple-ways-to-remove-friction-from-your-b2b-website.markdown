Most B2B websites act like obstacle courses. 

You want the customer to sign up or book a demo. But instead of a clear path, you give them long forms, vague language, and too many choices. This is called friction. 

Friction is the enemy of growth. If a visitor has to think too hard, they leave. 

Here are three ways to remove friction and get more leads today.

## 1. Slash your lead forms
Every extra field on your contact form reduces your conversion rate. 

B2B companies often ask for too much too soon. You don't need their phone number, company size, and job title to send a whitepaper or book a discovery call.

### How to fix it:
*   **Audit your forms.** Look at every field. Ask: "Do we actually use this data right now?"
*   **Use email enrichment.** Tools can find the company name and size based on a work email. You don't need to ask the user.
*   **Shorten the path.** If your goal is a demo, ask for an email and a name. That's it.

## 2. Eliminate "choice overload"
When you give people too many options, they choose nothing. 

Many B2B homepages try to do everything at once. They link to the blog, the careers page, product features, and a webinar. 

If your visitor has to hunt for the "Get Started" button, you have already lost them.

### How to fix it:
*   **Pick one primary goal.** What is the most important action for a visitor? Make that button a contrasting color.
*   **Simplify the navigation.** Move secondary links (like "About Us" or "Careers") to the footer.
*   **Use "wayfinding" copy.** Instead of "Products," use "Solutions for [Problem]." Guide them where they need to go.

## 3. Answer the "anxiety" questions early
B2B buyers are risk-averse. They worry about price, implementation time, and whether your software works with their current tools.

If you don't answer these questions on your site, the visitor won't contact sales to find out. They will go to a competitor who provides the answers upfront.

### How to fix it:
*   **Be clear about integrations.** Create a section showing the logos of tools you work with.
*   **Show the product.** Use short video clips of the product in action. Don't hide the interface behind a "Book a Demo" wall.
*   **Address pricing.** Even if you have "Enterprise" pricing, give a "Starting at" price or explain your pricing model. Transparency builds trust.

### Pro tip: the "one-breath" test
Read your hero section (the top part of your website) out loud. If you can't explain what you do and who it is for in one breath, it is too complicated. Cut the fluff until only the value remains.
