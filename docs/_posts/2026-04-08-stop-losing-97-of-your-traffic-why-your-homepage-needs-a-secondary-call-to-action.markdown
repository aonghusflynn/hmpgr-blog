Most B2B SaaS founders have a single goal for their homepage: get the demo booking. 

They design their entire site around one big, bright "Book a Demo" button. While focus is good, this strategy often ignores a harsh reality. Most of your visitors aren't ready to talk to sales yet. 

According to various industry benchmarks, only about 3% of your market is "active" and looking to buy right now. If you only offer a high-friction "Request a Demo" button, you are ignoring the other 97%. 

Here is how to use a secondary Call to Action (CTA) to capture those leads before they bounce.

## The Problem: The "Marriage on the First Date" Trap

Asking a first-time visitor to jump into a 30-minute sales call is a big ask. It requires time, effort, and social energy. 

If they are just in the "problem-aware" stage, they want information, not a pitch. If your only option is "Demo" or "Nothing," most people will choose "Nothing." 

You need a middle ground. You need a way to stay in touch while they continue their research.

## How to Build an Effective Secondary CTA

A secondary CTA isn't just a second button. It is a lower-friction path for visitors who like your message but aren't ready for a commitment.

### 1. Reduce the Friction
While your primary CTA (the demo) is high-friction, your secondary CTA should be effortless. 
*   **Primary:** Book a Demo (Requires a calendar sync and a meeting).
*   **Secondary:** Download a Template or Watch a 2-minute Video (Requires an email or just a click).

### 2. Solve a Small Problem Fast
The best secondary CTAs offer immediate value. Don't just offer a generic "Newsletter." Offer a tool.
*   "Download our ROI calculator."
*   "Get the 10-point checklist for [Industry Problem]."
*   "Take a free self-guided interactive tour."

### 3. Master the Visual Hierarchy
You don't want your secondary CTA to distract from your primary goal. They should look different.
*   **Primary Button:** Bold, solid color (e.g., bright blue or orange).
*   **Secondary Button:** Ghost button style (an outline with clear text) or a simple text link with an arrow.

This tells the user’s brain: "This is the main path, but here is an alternative."

## Where to Place Your Secondary CTA

Don't just hide it in the footer. Use it strategically across the page:

*   **In the Hero Section:** Place it right next to your primary button.
*   **The "Mid-Page" Break:** After you explain a specific pain point, offer a resource that helps solve it.
*   **The "Exit" Section:** At the very bottom of the page, for those who scrolled all the way down but still aren't hitting the demo button.

## Pro Tip: The "Value-First" Video
If you don't have a lead magnet or a whitepaper ready, use a **"How it Works"** video as your secondary CTA. Label it "See the product in 90 seconds." 

It builds massive trust. It shows you aren't hiding the product behind a sales wall. Many users will watch the video, realize the product is exactly what they need, and *then* feel confident enough to book the demo.

## Is Your Homepage Leaking Leads?

You are spending money and time driving traffic to your site. Don't let 97% of that effort go to waste because you only gave them one way to win.

Give your visitors a choice. Give them value. Watch your conversion rates climb.
