B2B buyers are busier than ever. 

When a prospect lands on your website, you have exactly five seconds to win them over. If they can't figure out what you do and how you help them in that window, they will leave. 

Most B2B websites fail this test. They hide their value behind vague headlines and corporate fluff. 

Clarify your messaging to keep your visitors from hitting the back button.

## Stop hiding behind jargon

Many founders think "enterprise-grade" or "innovative solutions" sound impressive. In reality, these words mean nothing to your buyer. 

Jargon creates friction. Friction kills conversions. 

If your headline says, "Using synergistic data to improve your business," your visitor is already confused. 

Instead, speak like a human. Tell them exactly what your product does. 

## The three questions your homepage must answer

To pass the 5-second test, your "above the fold" section (the part of the site visible before scrolling) must answer three questions:

### 1. What do you actually sell?
Be literal. If you sell accounting software for consultants, say "Accounting software for consultants." Avoid vague terms like "financial empowerment suite."

### 2. Who is it for?
Call out your niche. Whether it's a software company or a local plumber, make sure they know they are in the right place.

### 3. What is the primary benefit?
What is the one big win the customer gets? Is it saving 10 hours a week? Is it increasing revenue by 20%? Focus on the outcome.

## How to run your own 5-second test

You don't need expensive software to see if your site works. You just need a few sets of fresh eyes.

1. Find someone who isn't familiar with your daily operations.
2. Show them your homepage for exactly five seconds.
3. Close the laptop or turn off the monitor.
4. Ask them: "What does this company do, and who is it for?"

If they can't answer accurately, your messaging is failing you.

## Actionable steps for a high-converting headline

Clear headlines outperform "clever" headlines every single time. 

*   Audit your current headline for verbs and target audience, then strip out adjectives like "best" or "fastest." Let your results prove those things.
*   Use the "So What?" test: Read your headline. If the natural response is "So what?", you need to get more specific about the benefit.

### Pro tip: Use your customers' words
Stop trying to write copy from scratch. Go to your latest customer reviews or support tickets. Look at the exact phrases they use to describe their problems. Use those exact words in your headlines. Your prospects will feel like you are reading their minds.
