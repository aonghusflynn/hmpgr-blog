Most B2B "About Us" pages are a graveyard of missed opportunities. 

They usually follow a predictable pattern: a blurry photo of the office, a long-winded history of the company founded in 2014, and a generic mission statement about "innovation."

Here is the truth: Your customers don't actually care about your history. They care about their own future. 

To convert visitors into leads, your "About" page needs to stop being a biography and start being a bridge.

## The Ego Trap: Why Most B2B Pages Fail

In B2B SaaS and services, trust is the primary currency. When a prospect clicks your "About" link, they are looking for one thing: proof that you understand their problem and have the character to solve it.

If your page is just a list of your achievements, you are falling into the ego trap. You are making yourself the hero of the story. 

On a high-converting website, **the customer is the hero.** You are the guide.

## 3 Steps to Refocus Your Story

### 1. Start With the Problem You Solve
Instead of "We were founded to build great software," try "We realized that marketing managers were spending 10 hours a week on manual reporting, so we built a way to reclaim that time." 

Lead with the pain point. It shows you are listening.

### 2. Showcase Your "Why" Through Their Lens
Your mission statement shouldn't be about being "the #1 provider." It should be about the impact you have on your clients. 
*   **Bad:** "Our mission is to lead the industry in cloud security."
*   **Good:** "Our mission is to ensure IT directors never have to worry about a data breach on their watch."

### 3. Humanize with a Purpose
B2B buyers are risk-averse. They want to know there are real people behind the screen who will answer the phone if something goes wrong. Use real photos of your team, but keep the copy focused on how those people serve the client.

## Actionable Advice: The "So What?" Audit

Go to your "About" page right now. Read every paragraph out loud. After each sentence, ask yourself: *"So what?"*

If a sentence doesn't explain how your experience, team, or values help the customer get closer to their goal, delete it or rewrite it.

*   **Before:** "We have 50 employees across three time zones." (So what?)
*   **After:** "With a global team across three time zones, we provide 24/7 support so your operations never stop."

### Pro Tip: The "Evidence of Success" Section
Don't just tell people you are trustworthy; show them. Add a small "By the Numbers" section to your About page. Instead of years in business, focus on:
*   Total hours saved for clients.
*   Total ROI generated.
*   Number of problems solved.
Specific numbers build more trust than vague adjectives.
