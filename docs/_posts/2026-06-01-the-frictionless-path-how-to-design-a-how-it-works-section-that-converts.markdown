You've captured their attention with a great hero section. Your social proof proves you are legitimate. Then, the momentum stops. 

Potential customers often leave B2B websites because they can't visualize the next step. They understand what you do but not how it happens. This uncertainty creates friction. 

In B2B SaaS, friction is a conversion killer. To fix this, you need a clear "How It Works" section.

## Why clarity trumps features
Most founders spend too much time explaining their product's technical architecture. Customers care about their own workload, not your backend.

A great "How It Works" section is a roadmap to a better future. It should answer one question: "How much effort will this take from me?"

## The rule of three
When explaining your process, stick to three steps. 

Three is a magic number for the human brain. It feels like a complete journey without being overwhelming. If your process has ten steps, group them. 

### 1. The onboarding (the setup)
Show them how easy it is to start. 
*Example: "Connect your CRM in two clicks."*

### 2. The action (the work)
Explain what your tool does automatically. 
*Example: "Our AI scans your data for hidden patterns."*

### 3. The outcome (the success)
Focus on the result they get. 
*Example: "Receive a ready-to-use report every Monday morning."*

## Use active language
Avoid passive voice. Start every step with a strong verb. 

Use words like:
*   Connect
*   Upload
*   Launch
*   Receive
*   Grow

Active verbs put the reader in control. The process feels fast and decisive.

## Visuals over text
Don't just write about your software. Show it. 

Use simplified UI sketches rather than full screenshots. Full screenshots change often and look cluttered. A simplified graphic, sometimes called "lollipoping," draws the eye to the specific button or result that matters.

### Pro tip: the before-and-after toggle
If your product solves a complex problem, place a small before-and-after visual next to your "How It Works" section. Show a messy spreadsheet on the left and a clean hmpgr dashboard on the right. This contrast reinforces the value of your process immediately.

## Make the next step obvious
The "How It Works" section should lead directly to a call to action (CTA). 

Confidence peaks once the reader understands the process. This is the moment to ask for a sign-up or demo. Don't make them scroll back to the top of the page to find the button.
