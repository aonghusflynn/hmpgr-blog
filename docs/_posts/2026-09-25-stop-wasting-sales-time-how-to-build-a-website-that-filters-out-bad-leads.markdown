Most B2B founders think more leads are always better. 

They focus on widening the top of the funnel and want every visitor to click "Book a Demo." But if your sales team spends half their day talking to people who can't afford you or don't need your specific solution, your website is failing.

A high-converting website attracts the right visitors and filters out the rest.

Use self-selection to clean your pipeline and increase your closing rate.

## Be brutally specific about your "who"

Vague headlines like "The Ultimate Platform for Growth" mean nothing. They invite everyone and fill your inbox with low-quality leads.

If you build software specifically for HR managers in mid-sized manufacturing firms, say that. Put it right in the hero section.

When the right person sees their job title or industry on your page, they feel understood. When the wrong person sees it, they leave. That is a win for both of you.

## Use "anti-features" to narrow the scope

Marketing usually focuses on what a product can do. To filter leads, you should mention what it doesn't do.

If your software is designed for enterprise security and has no "self-serve" tier for freelancers, state it clearly. You can do this in a "Who this is for / Who this is not for" section.

This prevents the "I thought it did [X]" conversation thirty minutes into a discovery call.

## Stop hiding your pricing

A major B2B time-waster is price shock.

You don't have to list every cent, but you should provide a starting point or a "starting at $X" bracket. If your solution starts at $2,000 a month, someone with a $50 budget needs to know that immediately.

Transparency builds trust. It also ensures every lead that reaches out is already comfortable with your price range.

## Ask harder questions on your forms

Short forms get more submissions. Long forms get better customers.

If you are drowning in bad leads, add one or two "friction" questions to your contact form:
*   "What is your annual budget for this project?"
*   "How many employees will be using the tool?"
*   "What is your biggest roadblock right now?"

Serious people will take the extra ten seconds to answer. Tire-kickers will fall off.

## Pro tip: the "not for you" section

Add a small section near your pricing or FAQ titled: **"We are probably not a fit if..."**

List 3-4 criteria that would make a customer a bad match. For example:
*   "You need a solution that integrates with 15-year-old legacy hardware."
*   "You have a team of fewer than 10 people."
*   "You are looking for a one-time project rather than a long-term partnership."

This level of honesty is refreshing. It gives your "perfect fit" customers more confidence that they are in the right place.

## Actionable steps to take today

1.  **Audit your headline:** Does it mention your specific target industry or role?
2.  **Check your CTA:** Are you asking everyone to "Book a Demo," or can you offer a "Self-Service Trial" for smaller leads to keep them off your sales calendar?
3.  **Update your FAQ:** Add a question about who your product is not designed for.

Your website should be your best SDR. It should qualify leads and hand off only the best opportunities to your team.
