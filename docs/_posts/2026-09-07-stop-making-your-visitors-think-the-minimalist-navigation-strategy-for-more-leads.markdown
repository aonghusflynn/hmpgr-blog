Your website navigation needs a clear purpose.

Many B2B companies treat their header menu as a place to store everything they have built. They include links to "Our History", "Partner Portals", and specific product features.

When you give a visitor too many choices, they often choose nothing. This is called decision paralysis. If you want more leads, you need to simplify the map.

## The rule of five

A high-converting B2B website should rarely have more than five items in the main navigation. 

Your goal is to guide the visitor toward a single action. Every link that doesn't support that goal is a potential exit ramp.

### What to keep
- **Product/Service:** What do you do?
- **Pricing:** B2B buyers want to know if they can afford you before they talk to you.
- **Resources/Blog:** Show that you are an expert in your field.
- **About/Company:** Establish trust and show the people behind the brand.

### What to move to the footer
- Career pages.
- Privacy policies.
- Support documentation.
- Social media icons.

## Use action-oriented labels

Avoid vague jargon like "Solutions" or "Offerings." These words are invisible to a busy marketing manager. 

Instead, use labels that tell the visitor exactly what they will find. "How it Works" is better than "Our Methodology." "Customer Stories" is more effective than "Success."

When labels are clear, users feel confident clicking. When they feel confident, they stay on your site longer.

## The "primary action" hierarchy

Your header should have one button that stands out from everything else. This is your primary call to action (CTA).

This is usually "Get a Demo" or "Start Free Trial." 

### The ghost button trick
If you need a secondary action, like "Log In", use a text link or a "ghost button" (a button with a transparent background and a thin border). 

This ensures the visitor's eye is naturally drawn to the solid, brightly colored primary button. This visually shows them the most important next step.

## Pro tip: the 1% rule
Check your website analytics for the last 30 days. If a link in your main navigation is being clicked by less than 1% of your visitors, remove it. Move it to the footer or tuck it inside a sub-menu. This instantly reduces clutter and increases the click-through rate on your most important pages.
