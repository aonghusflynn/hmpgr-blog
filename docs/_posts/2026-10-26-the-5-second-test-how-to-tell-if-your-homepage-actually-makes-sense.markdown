In the B2B world, your website has five seconds to prove its worth. 

If a visitor cannot determine what you do and who you do it for in that time, they leave. They will not dig through your About page or click your Features dropdown; they simply bounce.

Most B2B websites fail this test by prioritizing cleverness over clarity. They use vague headlines like "Empowering your digital future" or "Synergizing your workflow." 

To convert visitors into leads, you must be practical.

## The three questions your homepage must answer

Every visitor arrives at your site with three subconscious questions. You must answer them "above the fold" to keep the sale.

### 1. What do you actually sell?
Avoid jargon. If you sell CRM software for dental clinics, say "CRM software for dental clinics." Use plain language that a ten-year-old can understand.

### 2. How does it make my life better?
B2B buyers want outcomes. Tell them the results immediately, such as saving 10 hours a week or increasing revenue by 20%.

### 3. How do I get started?
Your Call to Action (CTA) should be easy to find. "Get Started," "Start Free Trial," or "View Pricing" are clear. "Learn More" is weak because it suggests more work for the visitor.

## Designing for scanners, not readers

People scan websites in an F-shaped pattern instead of reading word-for-word. 

To accommodate scanners, use a clear visual hierarchy. Large headlines work for your value proposition and subheadlines provide context. Bullet points help break up blocks of text.

A wall of text hurts your conversion rate. Use white space to direct the eye to your CTA.

## The power of the self-selection hook

Effective B2B websites help the right people stay while filtering out the wrong ones. 

Include a line of text that calls out your ideal customer. For example: "Built specifically for SaaS marketing teams with 10+ employees." 

When the right prospect sees that, they feel they have found a solution for their specific pains. This builds trust.

### Pro tip: the blur test
Take a screenshot of your homepage and apply a heavy blur filter to it. If you cannot identify the main button or headline, your layout is too cluttered. Your Call to Action should be the most visually distinct element on the page.

## Stop guessing and start optimizing

Your website is your 24/7 salesperson. If it is not converting, your message is likely muffled by complexity. 

Optimization means removing the friction that prevents customers from saying "yes."
