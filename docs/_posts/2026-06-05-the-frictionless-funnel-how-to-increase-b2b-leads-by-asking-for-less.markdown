You spend thousands of dollars on ads and hundreds of hours on content. 
A potential customer finally clicks. They land on your site. They like what they see. 
They click your "Request a Demo" button. 

Then they see it: a form with 12 mandatory fields. 
Company size, job title, annual revenue, and phone number. 
They close the tab. 

You lost a lead and the resources spent to acquire them. 
In B2B, companies often prioritize data over the user's need for a smooth experience. 
Change the approach.

## The psychology of form friction

Every field you add to a form is a hurdle. 
Each hurdle gives your prospect a reason to rethink their decision. 
In B2B, the cost of a conversion includes time and privacy. 

If your form looks like a tax return, people will treat it like one. 
They will procrastinate or abandon it. 
Reducing friction makes the "ask" proportional to the "value" you provide.

## How to audit your current forms

Look at your primary contact or demo form. 
Ask these questions for every field:

1. **Do we use this data immediately?** If you don't use "Company Size" to route the lead in real-time, stop asking for it.
2. **Can we find this elsewhere?** Tools like Clearbit or Apollo find LinkedIn profiles or company revenue based on an email address.
3. **Is it a deal-breaker?** If you would still talk to a lead who left the "Phone Number" blank, make the field optional or remove it.

### Shorten the path to "submit"

Research shows that reducing form fields from six to three can increase conversion rates by over 50%. 
Stick to the essentials:
*   Work Email.
*   First Name.
*   The one question that helps you prepare for the call.

## The power of multi-step logic

If you must collect a lot of information, don't show it all at once. 
Use a multi-step form. 

Start with the easiest question. This is usually something about their goals. 
Once a user starts a process, they are more likely to finish it. 
This is called the "Endowed Progress Effect." 

By the time they reach the email field on step three, they have already invested time. 
They are likely to hit submit.

### Use smart defaults and clear labels

Don't make people think. 
Use clear labels above the fields, not placeholder text inside them. 
Inside-field text disappears when people start typing. This causes confusion. 
Ensure your "Submit" button says something descriptive, like "Get My Demo" or "See the Tool."

### Pro tip: the "work email" filter
Don't ask for "Company Name." Instead, use a validation script that requires a work email address and blocks @gmail or @yahoo. Most lead enrichment tools pull the company name and industry automatically from the domain. This saves your user a keystroke and keeps your data clean.

## Action items

To start seeing more leads today, follow these steps:

1.  **Delete one field:** Choose the field with the lowest utility and remove it.
2.  **Test your mobile view:** Open your form on your phone. If it is hard to tap the fields, your mobile conversion rate is low.
3.  **Check your confirmation page:** Once they submit, tell them exactly when to expect a response. Uncertainty kills conversions.

A B2B website is a bridge. 
By asking for less, you show that you value your prospect's time. 
This is the first step in building a business relationship.
