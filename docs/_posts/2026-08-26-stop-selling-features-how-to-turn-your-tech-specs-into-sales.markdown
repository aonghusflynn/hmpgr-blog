Most B2B websites are written by the people who built the product.

This is a problem.

When you build something, you love the features. You want to talk about the "Advanced AI API" or the "proprietary cloud architecture."

But your customers don't buy features. They buy outcomes. They buy a version of their life that is faster or more profitable.

If your website reads like a technical manual, you lose money. Fix it this way.

## The "so what?" test

When you write copy for your website, ask: "So what?"

If you say, "Our software has a real-time dashboard," the customer asks, "So what?"
The answer is: "So you stop wasting three hours a week on manual reporting."

The second sentence sells. The first sentence just explains how you get there.

### Features tell, outcomes sell
Features are the "what." Outcomes are the "why."

- **Feature:** 256-bit encryption.
- **Outcome:** Sleep soundly because your customer data is safe.

- **Feature:** One-click integration with Slack.
- **Outcome:** Keep your team aligned without leaving your chat window.

## Map the transformation

Your customer is currently at Point A (a place of frustration). They want to be at Point B (a place of success). Your website's job is to show them that your product is the bridge.

### Focus on the pain you remove
B2B buyers are often motivated by risk or annoyance. Identify the biggest headache your tool solves. Highlight that pain, then show the relief your product provides.

### Use the benefit-first headline
Most B2B headers are boring. They say things like "Our services" or "Features."

Replace those with benefits.
Instead of "Reporting tools," use "Make data-driven decisions in seconds."

## Speak like a human

Business-to-business is person-to-person. You are still selling to a human. Avoid "corporate speak" that says nothing.

- **Bad:** "We provide end-to-end scalable solutions for enterprise synergy."
- **Good:** "We help your team get more done without the extra meetings."

Short words win. Short sentences convert. If a middle-school student cannot understand what you do in ten seconds, your copy is too complex.

### Pro tip: the customer-voice hack
Go to your latest five-star reviews or your most recent support emails. Look for the exact phrases your customers use to describe their favorite part of your tool. Use those exact words in your headlines. Your customers are better at writing your copy than you are because they know exactly why they bought it.

## Audit your own words

Look at your homepage. Count how many times you say "we" versus how many times you say "you."

A website focused on "we" makes you the hero of the story. Your customer should be the hero. You are the guide who helps them win.

### How to start today:
1. Identify your three most "technical" feature descriptions.
2. Apply the "so what?" test to each one.
3. Rewrite them as benefits that focus on time and money.
4. Replace the old text and watch your engagement grow.
