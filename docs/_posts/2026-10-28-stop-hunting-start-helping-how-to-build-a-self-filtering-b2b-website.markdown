Most B2B websites are designed like wide-cast nets. They try to capture everyone.

The problem? You don't want everyone. You want the right people.

When your website is too vague, you end up with two problems. First, qualified leads leave because they aren't sure you can solve their specific problem. Second, your sales team wastes hours talking to people who can't afford you or don't need your specific features.

Successful B2B websites are filters. Build a self-filtering site to attract higher-quality leads.

## Speak directly to your industry

Vague headlines speak to no one. Visitors have to do the mental work to figure out if your tool fits their workflow. Most won't bother.

### Use industry-specific use cases
Create a "Solutions" or "Roles" menu in your navigation. Instead of listing features like "Automated Reporting," create pages for:
*   Reporting for Marketing Managers
*   Data Visualization for Data Analysts
*   Compliance Tracking for Operations Teams

When a visitor sees their job title or their specific industry on your site, their trust in you increases.

## Define your ideal customer

Confidence is attractive. High-converting websites are explicit about their ideal audience. 

Defining your ideal customer profile (ICP) clearly on your homepage saves your team's time. 

### Implementation
Include a section that defines your audience. 
*   **Good for:** Teams of 50+ or companies with an existing CRM.
*   **Not good for:** Solopreneurs or seed-stage startups.

This clarity attracts the right leads. It helps them feel they have found the perfect match.

## Use comparison pages to control the narrative

Your prospects are already comparing you to your competitors. If you don't help them do it, they will go to third-party review sites. 

On those sites, you lose control of the story.

Build "You vs. Them" pages. Focus on why specific customers choose you over industry giants. For example: "Why SaaS Founders choose Hmpgr over legacy enterprise auditors."

## The "micro-commitment" ladder

Not every visitor is ready for a 30-minute demo. If "Book a Demo" is your only call to action, you lose the 90% of people who are still in the research phase.

Create a path for different levels of intent:
1.  **Low intent:** A free tool or a high-value checklist.
2.  **High intent:** The "Book a Demo" or "Start Free Trial" button.

This keeps your brand in their mind until they are ready to talk to sales.

### Pro tip: use "negative constraints" in your forms
Add a single field to your contact form that asks for "Annual Revenue" or "Team Size" with specific ranges. If a lead selects a range that is too small for your service, redirect them to a helpful resource page instead of a "Thank You" page. This keeps your sales pipeline clean and provides value to the user. It also saves your team's time.
