Most B2B websites read like technical manuals. They list every tool, integration, and specification. They focus on what the product is.

Customers buy results, not tools. They buy a version of their business where a specific problem has disappeared.

If your website isn't converting, you likely have a "feature problem." You are describing the shovel when the customer wants to know about the hole.

## The difference between features and outcomes

A feature is what your product does. An outcome is what your customer achieves.

*   **Feature:** "Our software has 256-bit encryption."
*   **Outcome:** "Keep your sensitive client data safe from hackers."

*   **Feature:** "Automated reporting dashboard."
*   **Outcome:** "Save five hours a week on manual data entry."

B2B buyers are often overwhelmed. They don't want to do the mental math to figure out how your feature helps them. You must do that work for them.

## How to audit your own messaging

Look at your homepage right now. Read your headlines out loud. If your sentences start with "We," "Our," or the name of your product, you are probably feature-dumping.

### Step 1: the "so what?" test
Read a line of your copy and ask yourself, "So what?"

If your copy says, "We offer 24/7 cloud monitoring," the "So what?" answer is: "So your site never goes down while you're sleeping." That second sentence is what you should actually write.

### Step 2: identify the change
Every B2B purchase is a move from Point A (Pain) to Point B (Resolution). Your website should map out this journey.

Highlight the frustration of Point A. Then, paint a clear picture of the relief at Point B. Your product is the bridge between the two.

## Rewriting for impact

Don't delete your features entirely. They are important for the final stages of a sale. Instead, move them down the page.

### Use outcome-based headlines
Your H1 should be a bold statement of the primary value you provide.

Instead of: "The All-in-One CRM for Sales Teams."
Try: "Close More Deals Without the Admin Headache."

### Support with evidence
When you talk about outcomes, back them up with specific numbers. "Increase revenue by 20%" is more powerful than "Grow your business."

## Pro tip: steal your customers' words
The best copy isn't written; it's overheard. Look at your latest G2 reviews or support tickets to see how your customers describe their problems. Use their exact phrases on your website. When a prospect reads their own thoughts on your page, trust is built instantly.

## Does your website pass the test?

Successful copy is empathetic. It shows the customer that you understand their world.

Is your website clear about the outcomes you provide? Or is it hiding behind a list of features?
