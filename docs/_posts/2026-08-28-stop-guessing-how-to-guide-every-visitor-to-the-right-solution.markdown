Most B2B websites try to speak to everyone at once. 

When you treat a CEO the same way you treat a marketing manager, you lose both. They have different problems and different goals. 

If your website doesn't help them self-select their path within seconds, they will leave. Build a "self-selection" engine to turn visitors into leads.

## Why "one-size-fits-all" fails in B2B
In B2B SaaS, your buyer is a committee. 

IT directors care about security and integration, while CFOs focus on ROI. The end-user just wants something easy to use. 

If your homepage only talks about "managing workflows," you aren't helping any of them. Vague websites don't convert.

## Build the "choose your own adventure" path
Give your visitors a map. Do not make them hunt through your "features" page to see if you solve their specific problem.

### 1. Segment by industry or role
Add a "who it's for" section right below your hero area. Use clear labels like:
*   For founders
*   For agency owners

When a visitor sees their own title, they click. Once they do, you can serve them content that actually matters to them.

### 2. Solve by use case, not features
Features are what your product does. Use cases are what your product achieves. 

Instead of a menu item called "integrations," try "connect your stack." Instead of "reporting," try "get board-ready insights." Show them the finish line, not the shoes they need to wear to get there.

## The "breadcrumb" strategy for high-value leads
B2B sales cycles are long. Because most people won't "book a demo" on their first visit, you need a path for the "not-yet-ready" buyer. 

### Create low-friction entry points
If they aren't ready for a demo, find the next best step:
*   A 2-minute interactive tour.
*   A calculator that shows potential savings.
*   A case study specific to their industry.

Stay in their orbit by guiding them to these resources without forcing a high-pressure sales call too early.

### Pro tip: the "job title" filter
If you use a contact form, add a "job title" dropdown. Use that data to redirect them to a "thank you" page tailored to that role. Show a CEO a testimonial from another executive instead of a technical setup guide.

## Audit your journey
Is your website a maze or a guided tour? Most founders are too close to their own product to see the friction.

Clear navigation reduces the mental effort required to buy from you. If a visitor has to think too hard, you have already lost the sale.
