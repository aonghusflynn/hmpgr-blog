In B2B SaaS, many think they need to "convince" people to buy. 

We add copy, graphics, and features. But friction is the problem, not a lack of information.

Friction is anything that slows a visitor down or makes them think too hard. If your website feels like work, your prospects will leave. 

Here are three common friction points and how to fix them today.

## 1. Your forms are too greedy

Every field you add to a signup or contact form decreases your conversion rate. 

B2B founders often try to qualify leads by asking for company size, job title, and industry right away. This helps your sales team but hurts your growth. 

### How to fix it
Ask only for what you need to start the conversation. Usually, that is just an email address. You can collect the rest of the data later through enrichment tools or during the first discovery call.

## 2. The mystery meat call to action

Does your button say "Submit" or "Get Started"? 

These labels have low value because they do not tell the user what happens next. In B2B, uncertainty kills conversions. Prospects fear being sucked into a 60-minute high-pressure sales pitch.

### How to fix it
Be specific. If clicking the button leads to a calendar, use "Pick a Time for Your Demo." If it leads to a PDF, use "Download the Strategy Guide." 

Clear expectations build trust. Trust leads to clicks.

## 3. Decision fatigue in the navigation

Many B2B sites have 10 different links in their header. 

When you give a visitor too many choices, they often choose nothing. Your navigation is a guide rather than a library index. 

### How to fix it
Limit your main navigation to 4 or 5 items. Move everything else, like Careers, Press, or Privacy Policy, to the footer. 

Focus on the path that leads to revenue.

### Pro tip: the incognito test
Open your website in an incognito window. Set a timer for 30 seconds. Try to find your pricing and your demo booking page. If it takes more than three clicks or 20 seconds to find either, you have a friction problem. High-intent buyers want the fastest path to the price and the booking.
