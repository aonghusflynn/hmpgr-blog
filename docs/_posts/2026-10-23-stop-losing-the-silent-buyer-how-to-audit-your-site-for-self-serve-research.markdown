Modern B2B buyers have changed. 

Most of your prospects are 70% through their buying journey before they ever want to talk to a salesperson. They don't want a "discovery call" to learn your basic features. They want to find those answers themselves, late at night, without a calendar invite.

A website that acts as a gatekeeper loses leads to competitors who make information easy to find.

## The rise of the invisible lead

An invisible lead is a prospect who visits your site, looks for a specific answer, fails to find it, and leaves. You never even knew they were there. 

To convert these buyers, your website must be a self-serve research tool. You need to stop hiding your value behind "Contact Us" buttons. 

### Audit point 1: Answer the "how" and "how much"

The two biggest questions every B2B buyer has are: "How does this actually work?" and "How much will this cost me?"

Most B2B sites fail here. They use vague terms like "enterprise-grade solutions" and "scalable architecture." 

Instead, show the product. Use high-quality screenshots or short, looping videos. If you can't give an exact price, give a "starting at" price or a calculator. Transparency builds trust. Ambiguity creates friction.

### Audit point 2: The navigation stress test

Your navigation menu is your buyer's map. If it is cluttered, they will get lost. 

Look at your header. Does it tell the user exactly where to go based on their specific problem? 

Avoid generic labels like "Solutions." Use labels that reflect the user's role or industry, such as "For Marketing Teams" or "For Healthcare." This allows the "silent researcher" to find relevant content in seconds.

## Three steps to audit your content flow

To keep a researcher on your site, you must guide them from one thought to the next.

1.  **Identify your "money pages":** These are your pricing and feature pages.
2.  **Check the "next step":** At the bottom of every page, offer a clear next step beyond "Book a Demo." This could be a case study or a technical spec sheet.
3.  **Simplify the language:** Remove jargon. If a fifth-grader cannot understand what your product does, a busy CEO won't bother trying.

### Pro tip: The incognito audit
Open your website in an incognito window. Set a timer for 60 seconds. Try to find your pricing and a specific product feature. If you can't find both within one minute, your website is too complex. Your prospects will not give you more time than that.

## Build for the buyer, not the sales team

Your website is your best salesperson when it works 24/7. It should answer objections before they are even voiced. 

When you make it easy for people to buy, they will reward you with their business. When you make it hard, they will find someone who makes it easy.
