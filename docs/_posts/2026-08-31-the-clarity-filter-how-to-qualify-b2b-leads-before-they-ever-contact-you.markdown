Most B2B websites try to be everything to everyone. 

Founders often fear that being too specific will "scare away" potential revenue. In reality, being vague kills your conversion rate. When you speak to everyone, you resonate with no one.

If your sales team spends 40% of their time talking to leads who "aren't a good fit," your website is failing at qualification.

## Why your website should say "no"

A high-converting B2B website is a filter. It should attract your ideal customer and signal to everyone else that they are in the wrong place.

This is about being helpful. You save the "wrong" visitor time and save your team the cost of chasing a dead-end lead.

### The cost of generalization
When your copy is too broad, your value proposition gets diluted. 
- High-quality leads cannot tell if you solve their specific problem.
- Low-quality leads sign up for demos and waste your sales team's time.
- Your data becomes noisy. This makes it harder to see what works.

## Three ways to implement the clarity filter

You do not need to rebuild your site to start qualifying better. Use these structural changes to sharpen your focus.

### 1. Name your audience in the hero section
State clearly who the product is for. Instead of "The best project management software," try "Project management for mid-market construction firms." 

The moment a visitor lands, they should see that the solution fits their needs.

### 2. Be transparent about requirements
If your software only works with Salesforce, say that. If you only work with companies with more than 50 employees, make it clear. 

Listing integrations and company profiles shows expertise. It shows you understand the environment your customers live in.

### 3. Use anti-features
Sometimes, the best way to describe what you do is to describe what you do not do.
- "We do not do custom dev work; we provide a standardized platform."
- "Built for enterprise teams, not for freelancers."

Honesty builds trust with the people who need your solution.

### The pricing friction move
Even if you do not show exact prices, include a "Starting at" or "Typical engagement" range. If your service starts at $5,000 and the visitor has a $500 budget, you have saved both sides a discovery call that would have failed.

## Actionable steps for this week

1. **Audit your hero section:** Does it mention a specific industry or role? If not, add one.
2. **Check your lead form:** Add a qualifying question, such as "What is your annual revenue?" or "What CRM do you use?"
3. **Review your FAQ:** Add a question: "Who is this not a good fit for?" Answer it honestly.
