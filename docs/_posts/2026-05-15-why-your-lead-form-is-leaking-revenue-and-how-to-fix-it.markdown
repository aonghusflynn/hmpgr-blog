You have built a great product and your homepage explains its value. Traffic is growing. 

But then, the momentum stops. 

Visitors click "Get a Demo" or "Contact Sales," look at your form, and then leave. This is the "leaky bucket" of B2B marketing. If your form is too long or too confusing, you are paying for traffic that never turns into revenue.

Here is how to optimize your B2B lead forms for better conversion.

## 1. Eliminate "greedy" fields
Every field you add to a form decreases the chance of someone finishing it. B2B companies often ask for too much data too soon.

Do you really need their phone number and job title just to send a whitepaper? Probably not.

### Audit your fields
Look at your current form. Ask yourself: "Does our sales team actually use this data right now?" If the answer is no, delete the field. You can gather more information later during the discovery call.

## 2. Use "smart friction"
There is a difference between bad friction and smart friction. 

Bad friction is a confusing layout. Smart friction is a high-value question that helps you qualify a lead without exhausting them.

### Multi-step forms
If you must ask for five or more pieces of information, do not show them all at once. Break the form into two or three steps. 
* **Step 1:** Low-threat info (work email).
* **Step 2:** Qualification (company size or the problem they are solving).

This is the "sunk cost" effect. Once a user starts, they are more likely to finish.

## 3. Fix your call to action (CTA)
"Submit" is the most boring word in the English language. It sounds like work and giving up.

Your button should reflect the value the user receives.
* Instead of **Submit**, try **Get my free audit**.
* Instead of **Contact Sales**, try **See hmpgr in action**.

## 4. Provide immediate reassurance
B2B buyers are protective of their time. They do not want a salesperson to hound them five minutes after they fill out a form.

Add a small line of text under your CTA to set expectations:
* "We'll reach out within 24 hours."
* "No credit card required."
* "We hate spam as much as you do."

### Pro tip: The "work email" filter
If you have too many low-quality leads using Gmail or Yahoo addresses, add a validator that requires a "work email." This ensures your sales team only spends time on business inquiries. However, only do this if your brand is established enough to demand it.
