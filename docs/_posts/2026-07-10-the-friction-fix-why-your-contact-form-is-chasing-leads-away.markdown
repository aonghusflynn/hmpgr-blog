You did the hard part. 

You built a great product. You wrote a compelling headline. You even convinced a skeptical visitor to click your "Get Started" button.

But then, they saw the form. 

Ten mandatory fields, a "Company Size" dropdown, and a required phone number. Suddenly, that lead is gone. They left because you made it feel like work.

In B2B, we often kill our own momentum. Fix your forms to stop losing leads at the finish line.

## The cost of every extra field

Every time you ask for a piece of information, you pay for it in conversion rate.

Research shows that reducing form fields increases sign-ups. Yet, many B2B companies treat their contact forms like a background check. 

Ask yourself: Do you really need their job title right now? Do you need their phone number to send them a PDF?

If you don't need the data to fulfill the immediate request, delete the field. You can always gather more information later in the sales process.

## Focus on "low-stakes" entry points

The biggest mistake is asking for a marriage proposal on the first date. 

A "Request a Demo" form is a high-stakes ask. It implies a long sales call. For visitors who are just browsing, this is too much pressure.

Instead, offer a low-stakes alternative:
*   An email-only newsletter signup.
*   A checklist or resource.
*   A "Quick Quote" that only requires a URL and an email.

### Start with the easy stuff

If you must use a long form, use a multi-step layout. 

Ask for the easiest information first, like their name or company name. By the time the user gets to the "harder" questions, they have already invested time in the process. This "Sunk Cost" effect works for conversion.

## Make the buttons meaningful

"Submit" is the most boring word in the English language. It also sounds like a chore.

Your button should describe the benefit the user is about to receive. Use action-oriented language that mirrors the user's goal:
*   "Show me my audit"
*   "Get the free guide"
*   "Start my trial"

### Pro tip: use "smart" form logic
Instead of asking users to type their entire life story, use a clear "Company Email" field. Many modern tools can automatically "enrich" that data behind the scenes. They can find the company size, industry, and location based solely on the email domain. This keeps your form short for the user and your data rich for the sales team.

## Don't let tech glitches kill the deal

Nothing frustrates a lead more than a form that clears itself after a tiny error.

Ensure your form has "inline validation." A small red note should appear instantly if someone forgets the "@" in their email. Don't make them click "Submit" only to find out they made a mistake at the top of the page.
