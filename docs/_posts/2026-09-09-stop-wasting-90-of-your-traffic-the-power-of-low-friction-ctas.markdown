Most B2B websites have a leak in their sales funnel.

They focus entirely on the "Ready to Buy" visitor and plaster "Book a Demo" or "Contact Sales" across every page. While these are necessary, they only appeal to about 3% to 5% of your total traffic.

What happens to the other 95%? They leave and probably won't come back.

To grow your B2B SaaS or small business, you need to capture the "Second Wave." These are visitors who have the problem you solve but aren't ready for a sales pitch yet.

## Why "book a demo" can be a barrier

For a new visitor, "Book a Demo" feels like a high-stakes commitment.

It implies a 30-minute Zoom call and a follow-up sequence of sales emails. Most importantly, it implies they have to talk to a person before they understand your value.

If that is your only call to action (CTA), you force visitors to choose between a heavy commitment or leaving your site.

## Offer a path for the "just browsing" crowd

To increase conversions, you need a transitional CTA. This is a low-friction offer that provides immediate value in exchange for a smaller commitment like an email address.

Think of it as a way to prove your expertise before you ask for their time.

### Create tools instead of just text

Whitepapers are less effective now. Few people want to read a 20-page PDF on "The Future of Industry X."

Instead, offer tools they can use immediately:
*   **Calculators:** An ROI calculator or a "Cost of Inaction" tool.
*   **Checklists:** A 10-point audit they can run themselves.
*   **Templates:** A spreadsheet or framework that solves a specific piece of their problem.

### Focus on time to value

A good low-friction CTA should provide value in under two minutes. If your lead magnet takes an hour to consume, your busy B2B lead will save it to a "to-read" folder and forget it exists.

The goal is a quick win. Show them you understand their pain point. Solve one small part of it and invite them to the next step.

## How to place your low-friction CTAs

Don't hide your secondary offers in the footer.

If your primary "Book a Demo" button is in the top right or the hero section, place your low-friction offer in these locations:
1.  **Mid-page:** After you have explained the core problem you solve.
2.  **Blog sidebars:** Where educational intent is highest.
3.  **Exit-intent popups:** A final "wait, take this with you" before they leave.

### Pro tip: the reverse lead magnet

Instead of asking for an email to see the tool, let them use a lightweight version of your tool for free without signing up. At the end, offer to email them the results or a detailed report. This builds trust and leads to higher quality leads than a blind sign-up form.
