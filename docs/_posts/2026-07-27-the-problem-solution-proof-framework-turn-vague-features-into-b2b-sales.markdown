Most B2B websites talk about themselves. They list features and use words like "innovative" or "scalable."

Visitors care about their own problems rather than your features. To convert a visitor into a lead, bridge the gap between what you do and what they need.

The most effective way to do this is the Problem-Solution-Proof (PSP) framework.

## 1. Name the pain (the problem)

Before a customer buys, they need to know you understand their struggle. 

Define the problem to give your solution value. Identify the specific pain point your target audience feels every day.

### Speak their language
Use specific phrases like "Stop wasting three hours a day on manual data entry" instead of vague terms like "workflow efficiency."

### Be specific
Specific problems get clicks. Find the one thing that keeps your ideal customer awake at night and put that headline front and center.

## 2. Present the result (the solution)

Once the visitor feels understood, show them the "after" state. 

Show the outcome of using your software rather than listing every button. Focus on the result.

### Benefits over features
A feature is "256-bit encryption." A benefit is "Your data is safe, so you can sleep at night."

### Keep it simple
Use short sentences and avoid jargon. If a ten-year-old cannot understand what you do, your messaging is too complex.

## 3. Back it up (the proof)

B2B buyers are risk-averse because they spend company money and risk their reputations. 

They need to know you will not fail them. This is where strategic proof helps.

### Contextual evidence
Include short quotes mentioning specific results instead of only listing company logos. "Company X increased lead volume by 40% in three months" is more effective than a logo alone.

### Show the product in action
Use screenshots or video testimonials to build trust with real data.

## Pro tip: the "so what?" test

Read every sentence on your homepage. After each one, ask: "So what?" 

If a sentence does not explain how it helps the customer save time, make money, or reduce stress, delete it. Your website should be a focused path to a solution.
