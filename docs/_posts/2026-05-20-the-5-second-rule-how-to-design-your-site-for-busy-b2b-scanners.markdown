B2B buyers are exhausted. 

They don't read your website word-for-word. They hunt for answers. Most visitors decide whether to stay or leave within five seconds. 

If your homepage looks like a dense legal contract, they will leave. If your value is buried in a three-sentence paragraph, they will miss it.

To convert more visitors, design for scanners instead of readers. Use these techniques to make your site clearer.

## Why scannability matters for growth

The "Back" button is your biggest enemy in B2B SaaS.

When a founder or marketing manager lands on your site, they ask three questions:
1. What is this?
2. Is it for me?
3. What do I do next?

If they have to work hard to find those answers, you lose the lead. Scannability ensures the answers are obvious.

## Three ways to make your page scannable

### 1. Master the visual hierarchy
The most important information must be the largest. Your main headline should tell the visitor exactly what they get. 

Use subheadlines to explain how it works. Use body text only for the details. Most people will only read the headlines. Make sure those headlines tell a story on their own.

### 2. The power of white space
Clutter kills conversions. When you crowd your page with too many images and blocks of text, the eye doesn't know where to land. 

White space (the empty areas on your page) is a guide. It tells the visitor: "Look here. This is important." Give your buttons and headlines room to breathe.

### 3. Use bullet points for benefits
Paragraphs are for books. Bullet points are for websites. 

If you have a list of features, use a bulleted list. It breaks up the visual flow and makes the information easy to digest. 

## Actionable steps to improve scannability today

- **Shorten your paragraphs.** No paragraph on your site should be longer than three lines.
- **Use bold text sparingly.** Bold the single most important phrase in a section to draw the eye.
- **Use meaningful icons.** A simple icon next to a benefit helps the brain process the information faster than text alone.
- **Check your mobile view.** Scannability is critical on a small screen. 

### Pro tip: the blur test
Take a screenshot of your homepage. Apply a heavy blur filter to it in any image editor. Can you still tell where the Call to Action (CTA) button is? Can you see the path the eye should follow? If the page looks like a gray blob, your visual hierarchy is weak. Your CTA should "pop" even through the blur.
