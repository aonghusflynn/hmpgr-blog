Most B2B websites look like owner's manuals. They list technical specifications and integrations. The hard truth is that your customers care about their own problems. Focus your website on what the product does for the user to avoid leaving money on the table. To convert more visitors, talk about customer results.

## The 'so what?' test
Every sentence on your homepage should pass the 'so what?' test.

Imagine a skeptical prospect reading your copy. If you say, "We offer 256-bit encryption," the prospect asks, "So what?"

If your answer is, "It keeps your data safe," you are getting closer. If your answer is, "You will never have to worry about a data breach destroying your company's reputation," you have found the benefit.

### How to pivot from features to benefits
*   **Feature:** Our software has a real-time analytics dashboard.
*   **Benefit:** Spot falling margins instantly to protect your profits.
*   **Feature:** We offer 24/7 customer support.
*   **Benefit:** Avoid losing productivity to a technical glitch.

## Use your customers' own words
Your customers write the most persuasive copy. Look through support tickets, Slack, or review sites like G2 and Capterra. Look for the phrases your customers use to describe their pain points before they found you.

If a customer says, "I was tired of spending my entire Sunday on spreadsheets," use that. A headline like "Get your Sundays back" is more powerful than "Automated Reporting Tools."

## Audit your 'we' vs. 'you' ratio
Open your website now. Search for the word "we" and the word "you."

If "we" outnumbers "you" by a large margin, your website is self-centered. It tells the story of your company instead of your customer's success. Rewrite "we provide X" as "you get Y."

### Pro tip: the negative space strategy
Highlight the costs of the current situation. Sometimes, reminding a prospect of the cost of failing to solve their problem is more effective than promising a reward. Mention wasted hours, lost revenue, or manual stress. After you highlight the pain, your product is the obvious relief.

## Make the value impossible to miss
B2B buyers are busy. They scan your site during short breaks between meetings. If they have to hunt for the value proposition, they will leave.

*   **Keep headers short:** 6-10 words maximum.
*   **Use bullet points:** Don't hide benefits in heavy paragraphs.
*   **Bold the outcomes:** Make the "results" words stand out.

## Take action today
1. Pick your most important service page.
2. Rewrite the main headline to focus on a single, major result.
3. Replace technical features with emotional benefits.
4. Update your Call to Action to reflect the value (e.g., "Start Saving Time" instead of "Submit").
