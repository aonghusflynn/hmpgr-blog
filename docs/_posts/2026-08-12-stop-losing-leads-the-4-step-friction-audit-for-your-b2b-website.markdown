B2B buyers are busier than ever. They don't want to hunt for information. They don't want to solve puzzles. If your website feels like hard work, they will leave.

Friction is anything that slows a visitor down. It kills B2B conversions. Most founders focus on adding new features, but you should focus on removing obstacles.

Audit your site for friction to clear the path for your customers.

## 1. Trim your lead forms
Long forms are a major barrier. Every extra field you add reduces your conversion rate. 

Do you really need their job title, company size, and phone number right now? Probably not. You can gather that data later in the sales process.

### Actionable advice:
*   Limit your initial contact form to 3 or 4 fields, asking only for the email, name, and their primary goal.
*   Use "Smart Forms" to auto-fill data for returning visitors.

## 2. Stop hiding your pricing
B2B companies love to hide pricing behind a "Contact Sales" button. This creates massive friction. 

Modern buyers want to know if you are in their budget before they hop on a call. Even if your pricing is complex, provide a "starting at" price or a typical range.

### Actionable advice:
*   Create a dedicated pricing page that lists your main tiers clearly.
*   If you are enterprise-only, explain what factors influence the cost.

## 3. Kill the corporate jargon
Clear writing is a sign of clear thinking. Many B2B sites use "fluff" words like *synergy*, *next-generation*, or *optimized*. 

These words mean nothing to a tired marketing manager. They just want to know how you solve their problem. If a middle-schooler can't understand what you do, your copy is too complex.

### Actionable advice:
*   Read your homepage out loud. If it sounds robotic, rewrite it.
*   Replace vague adjectives with specific results.
*   Focus on the "Job to be Done."

## 4. Optimize for mobile decision makers
Your customers aren't always at their desks. They check your site on their phones between meetings. 

If your "Request a Demo" button is too small to click, you've lost them. If your site takes five seconds to load on a 4G connection, they won't wait.

### Actionable advice:
*   Test your site on a real mobile device, not just a desktop emulator.
*   Ensure all buttons are "thumb-friendly."
*   Compress your images to improve loading speed.

### Pro tip: The "one-click" rule
Every page on your website should have one, and only one, logical next step. Don't give your visitors five choices. Give them the best choice. Whether it's "Start Free Trial" or "View Demo," make the path forward unmistakable.
