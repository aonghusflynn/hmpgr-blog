You did the hard work. 

You optimized your SEO. Your value proposition is sharp. You drove a qualified lead all the way to your "Book a Demo" page.

Then, they saw the form. They saw twelve required fields, including "Company Revenue" and "Postal Code." 

They closed the tab.

In B2B SaaS, your form is the bridge between a visitor and a sales opportunity. If the bridge looks difficult to cross, your prospects will stay on the other side. 

Follow these steps to build a form that converts.

## 1. Audit your fields ruthlessly

Every field you add to a form decreases your conversion rate. 

Most B2B companies ask for too much too soon. You don't need a life story to start a conversation. You need an email address and a name.

Ask yourself: "Do we need this data right now to qualify this lead?" 

If the answer is no, delete the field. You can collect more data later through a discovery call or progressive profiling.

### Focus on "intent" fields
Instead of asking for a phone number, which often discourages users, ask one question that helps your sales team prepare. 
* Example: "What is your biggest challenge with [Topic] today?"

## 2. Ditch the "submit" button

Words matter. "Submit" is a technical command. It sounds like work. 

Your call-to-action (CTA) button should describe the benefit of clicking it. It tells the user exactly what happens next.

* **Bad:** Submit
* **Better:** Get My Free Audit
* **Better:** Watch the Demo Now
* **Better:** Book My Strategy Call

Keep the button color high-contrast. It should be the most obvious thing on the page.

## 3. Use single-column layouts

Our brains process information better from top to bottom. 

When you use multi-column forms, the user's eyes must zig-zag across the page. This creates cognitive load. It makes the form feel longer and more complicated than it is.

Stick to one column. It is clean, fast to fill out, and works well on mobile devices.

## 4. Give them "inline validation"

Nothing is more frustrating than hitting "Submit" and seeing five red error messages. 

Use inline validation. This shows a green checkmark as soon as a user types a valid email or a red message if they miss a digit in their phone number. 

It provides instant gratification. It lets the user know they are doing it right.

### Pro tip: The "micro-testimonial"
Place a single testimonial next to or just below your form. Use one that mentions a specific result. When a prospect feels a moment of hesitation before hitting "Send," that social proof gives them the final nudge of confidence they need.
