Most B2B websites make a common mistake the moment a visitor lands: they talk about themselves. They feature their platform or their software, but your visitor doesn't care about your solution yet. They care about their own problem.

To increase conversions, stop selling and start empathizing. Use the problem-first framework to fix your website.

## Why your current headlines are failing

Vague headlines are conversion killers. 

When a B2B founder or marketing manager lands on your site, they are looking for a tool to fix a specific pain point. If your headline says "Empowering the Future of Enterprise Excellence," they have no idea what you actually do. 

They won't stick around to figure it out. They will click the back button. To keep them on the page, you must mirror their frustration immediately.

## The 3-step information hierarchy

A high-converting homepage has a logical flow. It moves the visitor from "I have a problem" to "This is the solution."

### 1. State the pain point
Your hero section identifies the status quo that your customer hates. 
Instead of "The Best CRM for SaaS," try "Stop Losing Deals to Messy Spreadsheets." 

### 2. Present the "new way"
Once you have identified the pain, introduce your product as the solution. Show them exactly how life looks once that pain is gone. Use simple, direct language.

### 3. Provide the evidence
Now that they are interested, they will become skeptical. Demonstrate how you solve the problem using screenshots, metrics, or video.

## Design for the "skimmer"

B2B buyers are busy. They do not read every word on your page. They skim.

Make your website easy to digest:
*   Use descriptive subheaders.
*   Keep paragraphs to two or three sentences.
*   Use bullet points for features.
*   Make your Call to Action (CTA) stand out.

If someone only reads your headers, they should still understand exactly what you offer.

## Pro tip: the "so what?" test
Read every sentence on your homepage out loud. After each one, ask yourself, "So what?" If the sentence doesn't clearly explain a benefit or solve a problem, delete it. Your website should be a lean conversion machine. Avoid filling it with corporate jargon.
