Most B2B websites make the same expensive mistake. They spend 90% of their homepage talking about themselves.

They lead with "We are the #1 platform for..." or "Our AI-driven solution..."

Your customers don't care about your product. They care about their problems. To increase conversions, stop selling features and start solving headaches.

## The hero section trap

The hero section is the first thing a visitor sees. It is valuable digital real estate.

Most founders use this space to describe what their product is. 

*   "A CRM for logistics companies."
*   "Cloud-based HR management software."

This is descriptive but fails to motivate action. It forces the visitor to figure out why they need you.

## Flip the script: the problem-first framework

To convert more visitors, lead with the pain they feel at 3:00 AM. 

When a visitor lands on your site and sees their struggle reflected back at them, they feel understood. That feeling leads to trust and higher conversion rates.

### 1. Identify the main friction
Ask yourself: What is the single most annoying part of your customer's day? 

Is it manual data entry or messy spreadsheets? Lead with that.

### 2. Name the consequence
Show them you understand the cost of their current problem. 

If their data entry is manual, the consequence is wasted hours and human error. Mention it.

### 3. Position your product as the relief
Now you introduce your product. It is the relief to the pain you just described.

## How to rewrite your headlines

Look at this quick example.

**Before (feature-focused):** 
"Automated Invoice Software for Modern Agencies."

**After (problem-focused):** 
"Stop Chasing Unpaid Invoices. Get Paid on Time, Every Time, Without the Back-and-Forth."

The second headline works because it addresses a specific frustration and promises a result.

### Pro tip: use the 'so what?' test
Read your website headlines out loud. After each one, ask "So what?" 

If your headline is "We have 24/7 cloud support," the "So what?" is "I never have to worry about my site going down while I'm sleeping." 

The answer to the "So what?" is your real headline. Use that instead.
