You have five seconds to grab a visitor's attention. Most B2B founders obsess over that first headline. Consider what happens when the visitor actually starts scrolling.

Attention is the spark. Trust is the fuel. If your website doesn't prove you can deliver, your leads will vanish before they reach your "Book a Demo" button. 

Converting today's skeptical B2B buyer requires a "trust stack."

## Why your "features" list is failing you

Most B2B websites are a list of ingredients. You talk about your API and your dashboard. 

Customers buy the meal rather than the ingredients. Show what your customer becomes. Move away from technical specs and focus on the outcome.

## The three pillars of the B2B trust stack

To build a page that converts, you must layer your evidence. Use these three pillars to keep prospects moving down the page.

### 1. The validation bar
Place a row of recognizable logos directly below your hero section. Use companies your target audience admires or identifies with. This tells the brain, "People like me use this tool."

### 2. The specificity of results
Generic testimonials like "Great service!" are white noise. Replace them with "The Result Quote." 

A good result quote looks like this: "We reduced our churn by 22% in the first quarter using hmpgr." Specific numbers create a mental image of success.

### 3. The risk reversal
B2B buyers are afraid of making a mistake. They fear looking bad to their boss. Use your middle-of-the-page copy to address these fears directly. Mention your easy implementation or your dedicated onboarding.

## Stop making users think

Make your website feel like a smooth slide. Every section should answer one question: "What do I do next?" 

Don't bury your Call to Action (CTA) at the very bottom. Scatter it throughout the journey. If a user feels convinced after reading a case study, place the "Sign Up" button right there so they don't have to scroll back to the top.

### Pro tip: the "micro-copy" win
Add a tiny line of text underneath "Submit" or "Get Started" buttons to reduce friction. Phrases like "No credit card required" or "2-minute setup" can increase click-through rates by over 15%. This small bit of reassurance removes the final hurdle to conversion.
