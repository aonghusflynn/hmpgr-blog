Most B2B websites make the same mistake. They talk about themselves. 

They list their founding year, their infrastructure, and every feature they've built. 

Your visitors care about their problems more than your features. 

To increase your conversion rate, act like a solution rather than a technical manual. Here is how to audit your messaging for growth.

## Move from features to outcomes

A feature is what your product does. An outcome is what your customer achieves. 

B2B buyers want results. They want to save time, make more money, or reduce risk. If your website only lists '24/7 automated monitoring,' the customer must calculate why that matters.

**Do this instead:**
*   **Feature:** 'AI data analytics.'
*   **Outcome:** 'Identify your most profitable leads in seconds.'

Close the gap between reading the headline and understanding the value.

## The 'so what?' test

Every paragraph on your website should pass the 'so what?' test. 

Read a line of your copy out loud. Then, imagine a skeptical customer asking, 'so what?' If your copy doesn't have an immediate, benefit-driven answer, delete it or rewrite it.

### Focus on the 'jobs to be done'
Your customers 'hire' your software or service to do a specific job. 
*   Does it help them get home earlier or make them look like a hero to their boss?
*   Does it remove a manual task they hate?

Address these emotional and functional drivers directly.

## Speak like your customers, not your engineering team

Jargon kills conversions. Terms like 'synergistic paradigms' or 'end-to-end solutions' mean nothing to a busy marketing manager. 

Use the words your customers use. Look at your support tickets or sales call recordings to see how they describe their pain. 

If a customer says they are tired of losing track of invoices, use 'Never lose track of an invoice again' as your headline instead of 'Comprehensive Accounts Receivable Management.'

## Design for the skimmer

People skim B2B websites. 

Your value proposition must be visible; it disappears when buried in a long paragraph. Use clear headings and bullet points to guide the eye.

### Use the 'f-pattern'
Research shows users often scan pages in an f-shaped pattern. Place your most important outcome-driven copy in the top header and the first few subheadings. 

Make your boldest claims the easiest to find.

### Pro tip: the testimonial headline
Use impactful sentences from customer reviews as sub-headlines instead of hiding testimonials at the bottom of the page. Real customer language is often more persuasive than copywriter text because it carries social proof.

## Is your messaging working?

You can have a beautiful website, but if your copy doesn't resonate, your conversion rate will stay flat. 

Optimization is a continuous process. Start by changing one 'feature' headline into an 'outcome' headline today and see how your visitors respond.
