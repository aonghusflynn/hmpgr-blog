Most B2B websites treat visitors like they are on a scavenger hunt. 

You ask them to navigate complex menus and read long blocks of text. You make them fill out 12-field forms just to see a demo.

Friction is a bigger enemy than your competitor. It is anything that slows a user down or makes them think too hard. 

To increase conversions, clear the path for the traffic you already have.

## 1. Simplify your navigation
Your navigation menu is a map, not a junk drawer. Many SaaS companies try to list every feature and resource in their top header. This creates "analysis paralysis." When a visitor is overwhelmed with options, they often choose none.

### Focus on the essentials
Limit your primary navigation to the essentials:
*   **The product:** What it is and who it is for.
*   **The proof:** Case studies or results.
*   **The action:** A button for your main goal, like a demo or sign up.

Move other links, like your "Careers" page or "Terms of Service," to the footer. Keep the top of the page focused on the user journey toward a solution.

## 2. Audit your forms
Forms are where conversions die. Every extra field you add to a sign-up form decreases the chance of someone finishing it.

Do you really need their phone number, job title, company size, and annual revenue right now? Probably not. You can gather that data later in the onboarding process or through third-party enrichment tools.

### The "minimum viable form"
*   Ask only for an email address to start.
*   Use "smart fields" that recognize returning users.
*   State what happens after they click "Submit." For example: "We will send you a login link in 30 seconds."

## 3. Improve visual scanning
B2B buyers are busy. They do not read your website; they scan it. If your value proposition is buried in a long paragraph, they will miss it.

### Use the F-pattern
Structure your page to match how people look at screens. 
*   Place your most important message in the top left.
*   Use bullet points for benefits.
*   Break up sections with clear, bold headers.

If a visitor scans your page for five seconds and cannot tell what you do, your friction is too high.

### Pro tip: The back button test
Open your website on your phone using a cellular connection. If it takes more than three seconds to load, or if a giant pop-up blocks the screen, your friction is at a breaking point. Most users will hit the "back" button before they even see your headline. Prioritize speed and layout stability over fancy animations.
