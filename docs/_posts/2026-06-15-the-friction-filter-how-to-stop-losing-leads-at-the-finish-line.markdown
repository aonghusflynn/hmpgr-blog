You did the hard work. 

You drove traffic and built trust. The visitor is finally on your "Contact Us" or "Get a Demo" page. 

Then, they leave. 

Friction is the biggest conversion killer in B2B. If your website makes it hard for someone to take the next step, they won't do it.

Audit your conversion points and remove these hurdles.

## The hidden cost of curiosity

Many B2B founders want to know everything about a lead. They ask for company size, job title, annual revenue, and "how did you hear about us?"

Every field you add to a form creates a reason for a visitor to quit. 

Research shows that reducing form fields from five to three can increase conversions by up to 25%. You don't need their life story yet. You only need a way to start the conversation.

### Focus on the "big three"
For most B2B SaaS companies, you only need three things to start:
1.  **Work Email:** This identifies the person and the company.
2.  **First Name:** This allows for personalized follow-up.
3.  **The Main Challenge:** A simple dropdown or text box asking what they need help with.

You can find everything else during the discovery call or through automated enrichment tools.

## Kill the "submit" button

Look at your main call-to-action (CTA) button. Does it say "Submit"?

Nobody wants to "submit." It sounds like work. It sounds like sending a document into a black hole.

Your button copy should reflect the value the user receives. Use action-oriented language that promises a result:
*   Instead of "Submit," use **"Get My Audit."**
*   Instead of "Contact Us," use **"Start the Conversation."**
*   Instead of "Register," use **"Claim My Spot."**

## Design for speed

B2B buyers are busy. They often browse your site between meetings or on mobile devices. 

If your form is buried at the bottom of a long page or fails to load properly on a phone, you lose money. 

### Eliminate choice overload
Don't give your visitors ten different ways to contact you. If you want them to book a demo, make that the only clear option on the page. Too many choices lead to analysis paralysis and the "back" button.

### Use inline validation
Filling out a long form only to have the page reload with a red error message is frustrating. 

Use inline validation. This shows a green checkmark as soon as a field is filled out correctly. It gives the user a small win and encourages them to finish.

### Pro tip: the "thank you" page pivot
Conversion continues after the button click. Most "Thank You" pages are dead ends. Give the visitor something to do instead of just saying "We will contact you soon." Link to your best case study or a helpful video. Keep the momentum going while their interest is high.
