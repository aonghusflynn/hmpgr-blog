B2B buyers are busy. They scan your website like a grocery list rather than reading it like a novel.

If a potential customer can't understand what you do and how you help them within five seconds, they will leave. This is the "Skim Test."

Most B2B websites fail this test because they are too wordy. They hide their value in long paragraphs and "corporate speak." To grow your business, design for scanners.

## Why skimmability helps you grow

In the B2B world, your website is a tool to help a decision-maker solve a problem. They are looking for a reason to stay or a reason to move on to your competitor.

When you make your site easy to skim, you reduce the "cognitive load." This means it takes less brainpower to understand your offer. When things are easy to understand, people trust them more.

## 3 ways to make your site scannable

### 1. Write descriptive subheaders
Most websites use generic headers like "Our Services" or "Features." These tell the reader nothing.

Use headers that summarize the benefit. For example, use "Get Real-Time Insights into Your Sales Pipeline" instead of "Analytics." If a user only reads your headers, they should still understand your entire value proposition.

### 2. Kill the "wall of text"
Large blocks of text are where conversions go to die. No matter how well-written your copy is, a 6-line paragraph looks like work.

Break your text into smaller chunks.
- Use sentences that are 15 words or fewer.
- Keep paragraphs to 2 or 3 sentences max.
- Use bullet points to list benefits or features.

### 3. Use visual anchors
Your eyes naturally jump to things that look different. Use this to your advantage.

Use bold text for important phrases. Use high-quality icons to represent different points. Use white space (empty space) to give your words room to breathe. Guide the user's eye toward the most important information and your call to action (CTA).

### Pro tip: the "blur test"
Take a screenshot of your homepage. Apply a heavy blur filter to it in any image editor or just squint your eyes really hard. Can you still tell where the most important button is? Can you see where the main headline sits? If the visual hierarchy disappears when blurred, your layout is too cluttered. High-converting pages have a clear focal point even when they aren't in focus.

## Actionable steps for this week

You don't need a full redesign to pass the skim test. Start small:
1. **Shorten your main headline.** Make it under 10 words.
2. **Bold one important sentence** in every section.
3. **Turn one paragraph into a bulleted list.**
