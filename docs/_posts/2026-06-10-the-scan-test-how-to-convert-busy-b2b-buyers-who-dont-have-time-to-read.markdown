Your visitors are busy. They scan your website instead of reading every word.

B2B founders often spend weeks on copy. But if that copy is trapped in huge blocks of text, it stays unread.

If a prospect cannot understand what you do in ten seconds, they will leave. Pass the 'scan test' to turn skimmers into leads.

## Stop using wall-of-text paragraphs

Large blocks of text look like work.

Marketing managers calculate the effort required to read a page. If it looks hard, they skip it.

Break your ideas down. Paragraphs should be three lines or fewer. If a sentence feels long, cut it in half.

Short sentences build momentum and pull the reader down the page.

## Use headers that actually say something

Most B2B websites use generic headers like "Our Features" or "What We Do." These are wasted opportunities.

A scanner should understand your value proposition by reading only the headers.

Instead of "Analytics," try "Track your ROI in real-time." Instead of "Support," try "Expert help within 60 seconds."

Benefit-driven headers sell the product even if the reader skips the body text.

## The power of visual hierarchy

Not all information is equal. Your website should reflect that.

Use bullet points for benefits and bold text for important phrases. White space is a tool that directs the eye to your call to action.

### Focus on one goal per section

Each section of your page has one job.

Avoid explaining pricing and features in the same block. Dedicate a clear section to each. This makes the information digestible.

## Pro tip: the blur test

Open your homepage and squint until the text becomes blurry.

Can you still see the buttons? Is the flow of the page clear?

If the page looks like a gray blob, you need more contrast and better spacing. Your "Sign Up" or "Get Started" buttons must be the most striking elements on the screen.

## Fix your friction points

B2B buyers want the path of least resistance.

Cluttered sites create friction. Jargon creates doubt. The most successful B2B sites are easy to understand. Clarity wins.
