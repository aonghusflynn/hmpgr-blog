You've spent weeks perfecting your B2B homepage. Your headline is sharp and your product looks great.

But then, the visitor clicks your 'Request a Demo' button. 

They meet a form that looks like a tax return: twelve fields, three required drop-downs, and a request for a personal phone number.

Suddenly, they leave.

In the B2B world, friction kills conversions. If you want more leads, make it easy for people to talk to you.

## Why your current form is failing
Most B2B companies ask for too much information too early. 

You want to qualify the lead. You ask for their budget and company size, but every field you add reduces your conversion rate.

Research shows that reducing form fields from six to three can increase conversions by up to 66%. If you aren't using that data, you are leaving money on the table.

## Three steps to a high-converting form

### 1. Remove every non-essential field
Look at your form. Do you really need a last name? Can you find their company size on LinkedIn later? 

Start with just an email address. Keep other fields to a minimum. You can gather more details once the conversation starts.

### 2. Use 'smart' fields
If a user has visited your site before, don't make them type the same information twice. Use tools that remember their details.

Better yet, use an email enrichment tool. If they provide a business email, many tools pull their company name and industry automatically. This saves the user time and keeps your data clean.

### 3. Change your button copy
'Submit' is the most boring word in the English language. It sounds like work or giving up.

Instead, use benefit-driven language. Try:
* 'Get My Free Audit'
* 'See the Demo'
* 'Start Growing Today'

The button should tell the user exactly what they get when they click.

## Rethinking the 'thank you' page
The conversion continues after the user clicks the button. 

Most 'thank you' pages are a dead end. They say 'We will contact you soon.' In B2B, 'soon' usually means 24 to 48 hours. By then, the lead has already looked at your competitors.

### Give them an immediate next step
Instead of a generic message, give them something to do:
* Offer a case study.
* Embed a calendar link so they can book a meeting immediately.

## Pro tip: the 'work email only' filter
If you want higher quality leads without adding friction, use a validator that only accepts business emails (no @gmail or @yahoo). This filters out 'tire kickers' and keeps the form easy for serious B2B buyers.
