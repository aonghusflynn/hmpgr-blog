Most B2B websites read like an instruction manual.

They list technical specs and internal jargon. Focus on what the product does for the buyer.

In a crowded SaaS market, features are no longer the primary selling point. Your competitors likely have similar features. To win, sell outcomes instead of tools.

## Why feature lists kill your conversion rate

B2B buyers are busy. When they land on your site, they are looking for a solution to a specific pain point.

Technical specs require the brain to work hard. The visitor has to translate "Real-time API Data Sync" into "I won't have to manually export CSVs every morning."

If you make your customer do the mental heavy lifting, they will leave. Provide the conclusion instead of the data points.

## The "so what?" framework

To improve your website copy, use the "so what?" test. Look at every headline and bullet point on your site and ask that question.

### Step 1: Identify the feature
Let's say your software has "Automated Invoice Reconciliation." That is the feature.

### Step 2: Ask "so what?"
*So what?* The accounting team does not have to check payments manually.

### Step 3: Ask "so what?" again
*So what?* It saves them 10 hours a week and eliminates human error.

### Step 4: Write the result
Your new headline: **"Reclaim 10 hours of your week with error-free automated accounting."**

## Show, don't just tell

Once you have outcome-driven copy, you need to prove it. B2B buyers are naturally skeptical and need to see the result in action.

Use "In-App Proof" instead of generic stock photos. Show a clean dashboard that displays the specific outcome you just promised. If you promise faster reporting, show a screenshot of a report generated in seconds.

### Focus on "jobs to be done"
Your website should reflect the "job" your customer is trying to finish.
- Are they trying to get promoted?
- Are they trying to save the company money?
- Are they trying to avoid a compliance headache?

Address the job, and the conversion will follow.

## Pro tip: the "you" vs. "we" audit
Open your homepage and press Ctrl+F (or Cmd+F). Search for the word "We." Then search for the word "You." If "We" appears more often, your website is too focused on yourself. Aim for a 3:1 ratio in favor of "You." Shift the narrative so the customer is the hero. Your product is the tool that helps them win.
