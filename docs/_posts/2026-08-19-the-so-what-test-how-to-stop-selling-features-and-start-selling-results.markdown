Most B2B websites prioritize the builders over the buyers.

When you spend months developing a new dashboard or a faster API, you want to explain how it works. You list the specs and highlight the technical magic.

But here is the hard truth: Your customers care about their problems more than your features. 

If your website reads like a technical manual, you lose leads to competitors who promise a better future. To fix this, you need to pass the "so what?" test.

## Why features kill conversions

A feature is what your product is. A benefit is what your product does for the customer.

When a B2B buyer lands on your site, they are usually busy and looking for a solution. They do not want to do the mental gymnastics required to figure out how your "AI-powered data ingestion" helps them hit their quarterly targets.

If you make the visitor think too hard, they leave. You have to bridge the gap between your code and their career goals.

## The "so what?" framework

You can improve your website copy in thirty minutes by applying this simple framework to every headline and bullet point.

### 1. List your feature
Start with the raw fact. 
*Example: "Our software has an automated reporting suite."*

### 2. Ask "so what?"
Why does that matter to a human being?
*Answer: "Because it saves them time on Friday afternoons."*

### 3. Ask "so what?" again
Dig deeper into the business value.
*Answer: "Because it eliminates manual data entry errors and lets them send reports to their boss with one click."*

### 4. Rewrite for the outcome
Now, combine those into a benefit-driven statement.
*New version: "Stop spending your Fridays in Excel. Generate board-ready reports in seconds, not hours."*

## Focus on results

B2B buyers buy software to become more productive or less stressed.

### Change your headlines
Avoid headlines like "Multi-Channel Integration Support." It is dry jargon.

Instead, try: "See all your customer data in one place."

The first headline describes a technical capability. The second headline describes relief from the chaos of jumping between tabs.

### Focus on the "before" and "after"
Your website should show the visitor's current pain and the success they will achieve with your tool. 

When selling cybersecurity tools, focus on the peace of mind that comes from security instead of listing technical specs like "256-bit encryption."

## Pro tip: the "you" ratio
Open your homepage right now. Press Ctrl+F (or Cmd+F) and search for the word "we." Count the instances. Now, search for the word "you." 

If "we" outnumbers "you," your website is too focused on yourself. Flip the script. Rewrite your sentences to start with "you" or "your" to keep the focus on the customer's journey.
