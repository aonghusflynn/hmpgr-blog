Your visitors are not reading your website. 

In the B2B world, your potential customers are busy. They are scanning for answers, not looking for a novel. If your website looks like a wall of text, they will leave before they even find out what you do.

To convert more visitors, you need to design for the "Skim Effect." Here is how to make your most important information impossible to miss.

## The 5-Second Clarity Test

The average B2B buyer decides if they stay or leave in less than five seconds. 

Open your website right now. Look at it for five seconds, then close your eyes. Can you answer these three questions?

1. What do you actually sell?
2. Who is it for?
3. What is the very next step I should take?

If you can’t answer these instantly, your visual hierarchy is broken. You are forcing the visitor to work too hard. In B2B, if the visitor has to work, they go to a competitor.

## How to Guide the Buyer’s Eye

You cannot treat every element on your page with equal importance. If everything is "bold," nothing is. Use these three methods to direct attention to your conversion points.

### Use the "F-Pattern" for Scanning
Research shows that people scan digital content in an "F" shape. They read the top horizontal line, then move down and read a shorter horizontal line, then scan the left side vertically.

Place your most important value proposition in the top left. Place your primary Call to Action (CTA) in the top right. This aligns with how the human brain naturally processes a screen.

### Leverage White Space as a Tool
White space is not "empty" space. It is a functional tool. 

When you surround a button or a testimonial with white space, you tell the visitor: "Look here." Don't be afraid of gaps. Clutter creates confusion. Confusion kills conversions.

### Break Up Your Blocks
Large paragraphs are where leads go to die. Use these formatting rules:
*   **No paragraph longer than 3 lines.** 
*   **Use bullet points** for lists of benefits.
*   **Bold the first few words** of your most important sentences.

## Make Your CTA Impossible to Ignore

Your Call to Action should be the most visually distinct element on the page. 

Avoid "Ghost Buttons"—those transparent buttons with a thin outline. They are hard to see and even harder to click. Use a high-contrast color that isn't used anywhere else on the page. If your brand color is blue, make your button orange or green.

### Pro Tip: The "Blur" Test
Take a screenshot of your homepage and apply a heavy blur effect in any photo editor. Can you still see where the CTA button is? If the button disappears into the background, it’s not prominent enough. Your primary action should be visible even if the visitor's vision is blurry.

## Start Auditing Your Experience

Optimization isn't about making things look "pretty." It's about making things clear. 

When you remove the friction of reading and replace it with a clear visual path, your conversion rate will follow. Your buyers don't want to hunt for information. They want you to lead them to the solution.
