B2B buyers are busy. They are often looking for a solution to a specific problem under a tight deadline. 

If your website makes them work too hard, they will leave. They won"t tell you why. They will simply click the "back" button and visit your competitor.

Conversion optimization is usually about removing tiny hurdles that slow people down rather than launching massive redesigns. 

Here are three common friction points that are likely costing you customers right now.

## 1. The "feature-heavy" headline
Many B2B companies write headlines about themselves. They list technical specifications or internal project names. 

Your customers don"t care about your features yet. They care about their own problems. 

### Does your headline pass the "so what?" test?
Look at your current hero section. Read the main headline out loud. Now, ask yourself, "So what?" 

If the answer is "We have a faster database," you aren"t there yet. If the answer is "You will save four hours of manual data entry every week," you have a winner.

**Actionable advice:** Rewrite your headline to focus on the primary outcome your customer achieves. Use their language instead of your product roadmap.

## 2. Asking for too much, too soon
High-friction forms are lead killers. 

We often see B2B sites asking for a phone number, company size, and annual revenue just to download a simple PDF. This is a lopsided trade. The value of the PDF doesn"t match the "cost" of the personal data.

### Match the ask to the stage
A first-time visitor is rarely ready for a sales call. If your only call to action is "Book a Demo," you are ignoring 90% of your traffic.

**Actionable advice:** Reduce your form fields to the minimum. For top-of-funnel content, an email address is usually enough. You can gather more data once you’ve built trust.

## 3. The "hidden" pricing page
In B2B SaaS, there is a common fear that showing prices will scare people away. 

The opposite is usually true. Hiding your pricing creates anxiety. It makes the buyer think you are too expensive or that your sales process will be a long, high-pressure ordeal.

### Give them a ballpark
Even if you sell enterprise solutions with custom quotes, you should provide a starting point. 

**Actionable advice:** Create a pricing page. If you can"t give exact numbers, list your tiers and "starting at" prices. This qualifies your leads and builds transparency.

### Pro tip: the "stranger" audit
Ask someone who does not work in your industry to look at your homepage for 30 seconds. Ask them to tell you exactly what you sell and who it is for. If they struggle, your messaging is too complex. Real clarity should be understood by anyone.
