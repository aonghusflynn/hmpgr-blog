You've done the hard work. 

You perfected your headline. You added social proof. Your visitor is finally ready to act. They click your "Get Started" button, and then it happens.

They see your form. 

They see twelve empty boxes asking for their phone number, company size, and job title. Suddenly, they aren't interested. They close the tab.

In B2B marketing, your lead form is often the biggest bottleneck in your growth engine. Here is how to fix it.

## Why your forms are killing your conversion rate

Most B2B companies treat forms like a job application. They try to qualify the lead before the lead even knows if they like the product. 

Every field you add to a form decreases your conversion rate. If you ask for a phone number, expect a drop. If you ask for a physical address, expect a cliff.

### The psychology of "form fatigue"

Every empty box is a hurdle. Your visitor is subconsciously calculating: "Is the value I'm about to get worth the effort of typing this in?"

If you are offering a newsletter or a basic demo, a 10-field form is a bad deal. You need to balance the "ask" with the "reward."

## Ways to optimize your forms today

You don't need a developer to make your forms more effective. Focus on these areas:

### 1. The minimum viable data
Ask yourself: "What do I actually need to start a conversation?" 

Usually, it's just an email address. You can find their company name and job title later using tools or during the first discovery call. If a field isn't necessary for the next step, delete it.

### 2. Use single-column layouts
Our brains find it easier to process information from top to bottom. Multi-column forms, where fields are side-by-side, look cluttered and confusing. Stick to one clear vertical line. It feels faster to complete.

### 3. Change the button text
"Submit" is a boring, technical command. It implies work. Use benefit-driven language instead. Try "Get My Free Audit" or "See the Demo." Tell them exactly what happens the moment they click.

## Pro tip: the power of the multi-step form

If you must collect a lot of data, don't show it all at once. Use a multi-step form. 

Start by asking for one easy thing, like their website URL. Once they click "Next," they have already committed to the process. They are more likely to finish the remaining fields because they've already started. This is called the "Endowed Progress Effect," and it works for B2B sign-ups.
