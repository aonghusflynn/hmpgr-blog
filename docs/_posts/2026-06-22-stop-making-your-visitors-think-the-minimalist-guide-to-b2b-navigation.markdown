Most B2B websites treat their navigation menu like a junk drawer. 

They cram in product features, company milestones, and blog post links. While they intend to be helpful, they actually create "choice paralysis." When you give a visitor too many options, they often choose nothing. They get overwhelmed and leave.

To increase your conversion rate, simplify the path to purchase. Here is how to audit and fix your navigation.

## The rule of five

Your main navigation should rarely have more than five items. 

Visitors land on your site with a limited amount of mental energy. Every link you add competes for that energy. If your header is cluttered, your primary call to action (CTA) gets lost.

### What to keep
Focus on the essentials:
*   **The Product/Solution:** What do you actually do?
*   **Pricing:** B2B buyers want to know if they can afford you before they talk to you.
*   **Social Proof:** Case studies or "How it works."
*   **The Big Button:** Your primary CTA (e.g., "Get a Demo" or "Start Free Trial").

### What to move
Move anything that does not help a new visitor make a buying decision to the footer. This includes:
*   "About Us" or "Our History"
*   Careers
*   Press Releases
*   Privacy Policy

## Design for the skimmer

People scan websites rather than reading them. 

Your navigation should be legible at a glance. Use clear, descriptive labels. Avoid "cute" or "clever" names for your pages. 

If you sell "Inventory Management Software," call the menu item "Features" or "Product" instead of "The Magic Behind the Curtain."

## The grand slam CTA

Your primary call to action should be the most visually distinct element in your header. 

Give it a high-contrast background color so it stands out from other text links. When a visitor is ready to take the next step, they should find the button immediately.

### Pro tip: the ghost button strategy
If you have two buttons in your header (e.g., "Login" and "Book a Demo"), make "Login" a plain text link or a "ghost button" (a transparent button with an outline). Keep the solid, bright color for your "Book a Demo" button. This guides the eye to the preferred action.

## Actionable audit checklist

Spend 10 minutes today reviewing your header:
1.  **Count your links:** If you have more than six, move one to the footer.
2.  **Check your labels:** Use jargon-free language that a stranger would understand.
3.  **Test on mobile:** Ensure your "hamburger menu" works smoothly.
4.  **Verify the CTA:** Your main button must look like a button rather than a text link.
