Most B2B websites talk too much about themselves. 

Founders and marketing managers are proud of their products. They want to list every integration and technical specification. 

Visitors care about their own problems. 

If your website reads like a technical manual, you lose money. Use these steps to turn features into high-converting benefits.

## Why features fail to convert

Features describe what your product is. Benefits describe what your product does for the customer.

B2B buyers are often under pressure. They have a boss to answer to and a budget to justify. They want to save 10 hours a week on manual data entry.

Features appeal to logic. Benefits appeal to results. Show the visitor the result first.

## How to run the 'so what?' audit

Fix your website copy today with the 'so what?' test. This framework uncovers the real value of your service.

### 1. List your main features
Look at your homepage. Identify the items you highlight most. 
Example: "Our software has real-time API sync."

### 2. Ask 'so what?'
Imagine a skeptical buyer who looks at that feature and asks, "So what?"
* Feature: Real-time API sync.
* So what? Your data stays up to date across all platforms.
* So what? You avoid manual data entry and errors.
* So what? You can make decisions faster with reliable numbers.

### 3. Rewrite for the outcome
Take that final answer and make it your headline. 
Instead of "Real-time API sync," your headline becomes: "Make confident decisions with data you can actually trust."

## Focus on the 'before and after'

Your website should show the change your product creates. 

Show the visitor their current state: cluttered spreadsheets and missed deadlines. Then, show them the result: streamlined workflows and growth.

When you bridge that gap, the sale becomes easier. Sell a better version of their business.

### Pro tip: use the 'feature-benefit-proof' triad
For every major section on your page, follow this structure:
1. **The Benefit (Headline):** What do they get? (e.g., "Reduce Churn by 15%")
2. **The Feature (Body):** How do they get it? (e.g., "Our software identifies at-risk accounts automatically.")
3. **The Proof (Small Text):** Who else got it? (e.g., "Used by 200+ SaaS teams to retain revenue.")

## Keep your sentences lean

B2B buyers are busy. They skim. 

If your paragraphs are more than three sentences long, people will skip them. Use bullet points. Use bold text for emphasis. 

If a word doesn't help the reader understand the value, delete it. Clarity wins.
