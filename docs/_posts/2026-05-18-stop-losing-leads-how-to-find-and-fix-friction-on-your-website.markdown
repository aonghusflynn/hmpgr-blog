You spend weeks perfecting your product and thousands on ads. Visitors finally land on your site, but they leave without clicking a button.

The culprit is usually friction.

Friction is anything that slows a visitor down or makes them think too hard. In B2B, friction kills conversions. If your website is difficult to navigate or hard to understand, your potential customers will find a competitor who makes it easier.

Identify and remove friction to turn more visitors into leads.

## 1. Simplify your forms
Every extra field in a contact form decreases your conversion rate. B2B companies often ask for too much too soon. 

Do you really need their phone number and company size just to send a whitepaper? Probably not. 

### Audit your fields
Look at your primary lead capture form. Remove every field that isn't essential for the first follow-up. You can gather more data later in the sales process. Aim for three fields or fewer for top-of-funnel offers.

## 2. Eliminate choice overload
When you give people too many options, they often choose nothing. This is called analysis paralysis.

If your homepage has five different Call to Action (CTA) buttons, your visitor will get overwhelmed. They won't know where to start, so they will leave.

### Streamline the path
Identify the one most important action you want a visitor to take. Make that button stand out. Secondary actions should be less visually prominent. Clear paths lead to higher conversions.

## 3. Fix the wall of text
B2B SaaS founders love technical details. However, your visitors scan; they don't read. Large blocks of text are intimidating. They create cognitive friction because the brain has to work harder to find value.

### Use visual breathing room
Break your copy into small, digestible chunks. 
- Use bullet points.
- Keep paragraphs under three sentences.
- Use bold text to highlight benefits.
- Add white space between sections.

## 4. Clarify your navigation
Your menu should be easy to navigate. If a visitor has to click three times to find your pricing or About page, you have a friction problem.

### The 2-second rule
A visitor should understand where to go within two seconds of looking at your menu. Use standard labels like Pricing and Features. Don't try to be clever with your navigation titles. Clarity wins every time.

## Pro tip: the mobile thumb test
Open your website on your smartphone. Try to navigate and click your primary CTA using only your thumb. If the buttons are too small or tucked in a corner that is hard to reach, you are losing mobile conversions. Ensure every button is at least 44x44 pixels to keep the experience frictionless for mobile users.
