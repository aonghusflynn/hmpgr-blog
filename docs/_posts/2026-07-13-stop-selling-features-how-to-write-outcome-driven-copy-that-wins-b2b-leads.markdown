Most B2B websites talk about themselves too much.

They list integrations and workflows, but your customers care about their own problems, not your features.

Sell what your product does for the customer instead of what it is to increase your conversion rate.

## The problem with feature-heavy copy

Features are technical. They appeal to product logic rather than the buyer's needs. 

When a B2B SaaS founder or marketing manager lands on your page, they want a solution to a specific problem. They will leave if they have to work too hard to figure out how your "advanced analytics suite" helps them hit their goals.

Vague jargon creates friction, and friction kills conversions.

## The outcome-first framework

Shift from features to outcomes to fix your messaging. Use this three-step framework to rewrite your headlines.

### 1. Identify the "before" state
What is your customer doing right now that is painful, slow, or expensive? 
*Example: Manually exporting data into spreadsheets.*

### 2. Define the "after" state
What does their life look like after using your tool? 
*Example: Save 10 hours a week with accurate reports.*

### 3. Build the bridge
Write your copy to highlight that result. Instead of "We have automated reporting," use "Reclaim your Fridays with automated reporting that never misses a detail."

## How to audit your own messaging

Look at your homepage. Read your H1 and your subheaders. 

Ask yourself: "Does this describe a tool or a result?"

A headline like "The Ultimate CRM for Sales Teams" sells a tool. A headline like "Close More Deals Without the Data Entry Headache" sells a result. The second version converts better because it speaks directly to the user's desire.

### Pro tip: the "so what?" test
Ask "So what?" every time you write a sentence about your product. 
"Our software uses AI." *So what?* 
"It identifies trends faster." *So what?* 
"You can fix shipping delays before your customers even notice." 
Use that as your headline.

## Make it actionable

Don't rewrite your entire website in one day. Start with your hero section, the first thing people see. 

1. List your top features.
2. Write the specific benefit of each feature.
3. Turn those benefits into outcome statements.
4. Replace your jargon-heavy headers with these statements.

When you speak the language of your customer's success, they listen.
