Most B2B websites treat their "Contact Us" or "Demo" forms like a background check. They ask for a phone number, company size, industry, and a budget before even saying hello.

Every field you add is a hurdle. Every hurdle gives your potential customer a reason to close the tab. If you want more leads, stop making them work so hard to talk to you.

## Why your forms are killing your conversion rate

Friction is the enemy of growth. In the B2B world, we often think more data equals better leads. In reality, more fields mean fewer leads.

High-intent buyers are busy. They want a solution, not a chore. When a form looks long, the perceived effort outweighs the value of your product.

### The power of "just enough"

The goal of a lead form is to start a conversation. You don't need a life story to send a calendar link or a whitepaper.

Focus on "minimum viable data." Usually, that means an email address and a name. You can find the rest on LinkedIn once the lead is in your system.

## Three ways to reduce form friction

### 1. Kill the optional fields
If a field is optional, delete it. If you don't need the information to take the next step, it shouldn't be there. Optional fields clutter the UI and distract the user.

### 2. Use single-column layouts
Our brains process information better in a straight line. Avoid putting fields side-by-side. A single-column layout feels faster to complete and works better on mobile devices.

### 3. Smart defaults and auto-fill
Enable browser auto-fill. If a user can click once to fill their name and email, your conversion rate will climb. Make it easy for the browser to do the work for them.

## The "micro-copy" makeover

The text around your form matters as much as the fields themselves. 

*   **The Header:** Instead of "Contact Sales," try "Get a 15-Minute Strategy Session." 
*   **The Button:** Instead of "Submit," use "Get Started" or "See the Demo." 
*   **Privacy Trust:** Add a tiny line of text: "We hate spam too. Your data is safe."

### Pro tip: the "work email" filter
If you are worried about "junk" signups from Gmail or Outlook, don't add five more fields to verify the lead. Use a simple validation script that requires a business email address instead. This keeps the form short and helps you focus on serious B2B prospects.
